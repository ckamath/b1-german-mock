## 1. Contract law: can an AI agent conclude, cancel and withdraw from contracts for a consumer?

### Takeaway
German law has no AI-agent statute: an agent has no legal personality, so its declarations are attributed to the user (or to the operator acting as a human-law representative), and the user bears most of the risk of agent error. The first dedicated rule set is the Commission's Digital Fairness Act proposal of 11 Nov 2026, which is only a proposal and would not apply before roughly 2028.

### Cited Findings
Attribution of declarations (IN FORCE)
- §164 BGB requires a representative to make "a declaration of intent of their own" in the principal's name; German commentary concludes an AI system cannot be a Stellvertreter because it has no will and no legal capacity — [§164 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__164.html); [Händlerbund, Agentic Commerce](https://www.haendlerbund.de/de/ratgeber/recht/agentic-commerce)
- Botenschaft does not fit cleanly either: a messenger transmits a finished declaration, whereas an autonomous agent selects the counterparty and terms itself; proposed fixes in scholarship are the Computererklärung model (declaration attributed to the operator/user who deploys the system), the Blankett model (user issues an incomplete declaration that the AI completes) and analogous application of agency or messenger rules — [Händlerbund](https://www.haendlerbund.de/de/ratgeber/recht/agentic-commerce); [Mainz dissertation on Computererklärung](https://openscience.ub.uni-mainz.de/bitstreams/1ed26ed6-dfef-4f74-9457-f9f029efc748/download); [Börsen-Zeitung, Agentic Commerce und Payments](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/recht-kapitalmarkt/agentic-commerce-und-payments-was-ist-rechtlich-moeglich)
- BGH, 16 Oct 2012, X ZR 37/12 (online flight booking): automated declarations of a booking system are declarations of the operator, and are interpreted by how a human addressee may understand them, not by how the system processes them — [BGH X ZR 37/12](https://dejure.org/dienste/vernetzung/rechtsprechung?Text=X%20ZR%2037/12)
- A consumer who uses an AI shopping agent stays the contracting party and keeps consumer status; the AI is treated as a technical means of concluding the contract — [Händlerbund](https://www.haendlerbund.de/de/ratgeber/recht/agentic-commerce)
- An alternative, legally conventional structure is that the agent's operator (a company) acts as the consumer's representative under §§164 ff. BGB on a Vollmacht; the Vollmacht is in principle form-free (§167 BGB) — [§167 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__167.html)

Errors, avoidance, liability (IN FORCE)
- Avoidance is available for declaration or content errors (§119 BGB) and for incorrect transmission (§120 BGB); BGH, 26 Jan 2005, VIII ZR 79/04 allowed avoidance where software corrupted a correctly entered price during transmission — [§119 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__119.html); [§120 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__120.html); [BGH VIII ZR 79/04](https://dejure.org/dienste/vernetzung/rechtsprechung?Text=VIII%20ZR%2079/04)
- A party who avoids owes reliance damages (§122 BGB); a representative without authority is personally liable (§179 BGB) — [§122 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__122.html); [§179 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__179.html)
- The EU AI Liability Directive proposal (28 Sep 2022) was withdrawn: announced 11 Feb 2025, confirmed by the Commission in July 2025 — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/proposed-eu-ai-liability-rules-withdrawn); [Euronews, 31 Jul 2025](https://www.euronews.com/2025/07/31/eu-commission-confirms-ditching-of-ai-liability-and-patents-proposals)
- New Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 must be transposed by 9 Dec 2026; Germany transposes by a complete recast of the ProdHaftG (BR-Drs. 775/25, BT-Drs. 21/4297); software, including AI systems and SaaS, becomes a "product"; old law continues for products placed on the market before the cut-off — [IHK Frankfurt](https://www.frankfurt-main.ihk.de/recht/uebersicht-alle-rechtsthemen/vertragsrecht-und-e-commerce-recht/uebersicht/neue-regelungen-zur-produkthaftung-2026-7177184); [BT-Drs. 21/4297](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/042/2104297.pdf); [Heuking, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.heuking.de/de/news-events/newsletter-fachbeitraege/artikel/neue-produkthaftung-was-anbieter-digitaler-geschaeftsmodelle-jetzt-beachten-sollten.html)

Unilateral acts by the agent: cancellation and withdrawal (IN FORCE)
- A unilateral legal act (e.g. a Kündigung) by an authorised person is ineffective if no Vollmachtsurkunde is presented and the recipient rejects it without undue delay for that reason (§174 BGB) — [§174 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__174.html)
- Where a new provider, or a third party commissioned by it, is authorised to terminate the consumer's existing continuing contract, the termination or the Vollmacht to terminate must be in Textform (§312h BGB); the LG München I applied this against E.ON (17 HK O 11480/18) and held that the new supplier must actually hold the text-form authority — [§312h BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312h.html); [vzbv press release](https://www.vzbv.de/pressemitteilungen/finger-weg-von-meinem-stromvertrag-ohne-vollmacht-keine-kuendigung); [Verbraucherzentrale judgment database](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/urteilsdatenbank/energie/unzulaessige-werbeanrufe-und-vertragskuendigungen-28422)

Button solution, cancellation button, withdrawal button (IN FORCE)
- §312j(3)-(4) BGB: in consumer e-commerce the order button must be labelled "zahlungspflichtig bestellen" or equivalent, and the contract only comes into existence if the trader complies — [§312j BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312j.html)
- CJEU, 30 May 2024, C-400/22 (Conny): the button duty applies even where the consumer's payment obligation depends on a later condition — [CJEU C-400/22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/DE/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62022CJ0400)
- §312k BGB (since 1 Jul 2022): websites that allow consumers to conclude paid continuing contracts must provide a two-step cancellation button; if not properly provided, the consumer may terminate at any time without notice; financial services contracts are excluded — [§312k BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312k.html); [CMS, Gesetz für faire Verbraucherverträge](https://cms.law/de/deu/publication/das-neue-gesetz-fuer-faire-verbrauchervertraege)
- The cancellation button must also be offered where the contract was brokered through a third-party platform (OLG Hamburg, 5 UKl 1/23, energy supplier case, as reported) — [RA Kotz summary](https://www.ra-kotz.de/kuendigungsbutton-drittplattformen-klare-beschriftung.htm)
- Withdrawal button: since 19 Jun 2026, traders concluding distance contracts with consumers over an online interface (goods, services, financial services) must provide an electronic withdrawal function (new §356a BGB, implementing Art. 11a Consumer Rights Directive as inserted by Directive (EU) 2023/2673) — [Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/vertraege-reklamation/kundenrechte/widerrufsbutton-seit-juni-2026-onlinevertraege-einfacher-widerrufen-118449); [IHK Osnabrück](https://www.ihk.de/osnabrueck/recht-und-fair-play/recht/internetrecht/widerrufsbutton-ab-juni-2026-6975440); [Directive (EU) 2023/2673](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2673/oj)
- Further information duties apply from 27 Sep 2026 independently of the withdrawal button (as reported by IHKs; these stem from the Empowering Consumers Directive transposition) — [IHK Rheinhessen](https://www.ihk.de/rheinhessen/rechtundsteuern/gesetzesaenderung/widerrufsbutton-7034868)
- Directive (EU) 2023/2673 also gives consumers of distance financial services a right to adequate explanations and to human intervention where the trader uses online tools, and protects against dark patterns in financial-services interfaces; it applies from 19 Jun 2026 — [Directive (EU) 2023/2673](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2673/oj)

Contract terms (IN FORCE)
- Gesetz für faire Verbraucherverträge: in standard terms, after the initial term the consumer must be able to terminate at any time with at most one month's notice; notice to the end of the initial term may be at most one month; rule applies to contracts from 1 Mar 2022; insurance contracts are excluded — [CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/publication/das-neue-gesetz-fuer-faire-verbrauchervertraege); [§309 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__309.html); [Stiftung Warentest](https://www.test.de/Gesetz-fuer-faire-Verbrauchervertraege-Langlaufende-Vertraege-werden-fairer-5600636-0/)

Digital Fairness Act (PROPOSAL, 11 Nov 2026)
- The Commission tabled the Digital Fairness Act on 11 Nov 2026 (reported as COM(2026) 1100); it would replace the 2005 Unfair Commercial Practices Directive — [Lewis Silkin, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/11/11/european-commission-publishes-long-awaited-digital-fairness-act-102o9b5); [lovguiden.dk summary](https://www.lovguiden.dk/en/det-offentlige/eur-lex-kommissionsforslag/2026-11-11-digital-fairness-act-eu-seeks-to-ban-dark-patterns-and-loot-boxes-for-minors); [Euronews, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/11/eu-targets-subscription-traps-addictive-design-and-hidden-fees-with-new-digital-rules)
- It contains rules for AI agents that conclude contracts for consumers (reported as Art. 26): on first use the consumer chooses whether to confirm every contract, only those above/below a price threshold, or none; providers must let consumers set purchasing limits — [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/european-commission-proposes-the-digital-fairness-act); [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2548994); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/ai-agents-dark-patterns-digital-fairness-act)
- The annex of prohibited practices lists 62 practices, including 10 dark patterns, going beyond the DSA's platform-only ban — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/ai-agents-dark-patterns-digital-fairness-act)
- Subscriptions: easier cancellation of digital contracts, rules on automatic renewals and on free trials converting into paid subscriptions — [ecommercegermany.com](https://ecommercegermany.com/blog/digital-fairness-act-eu-proposal/); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/11/eu-targets-subscription-traps-addictive-design-and-hidden-fees-with-new-digital-rules)
- Enforcement: the Commission would get its own investigation and fining powers, with fines reported at up to 6% of turnover — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/eus-digital-fairness-act-would-let-brussels-fine-firms-up-to-6-of-turnover/)
- The proposal would not apply earlier than twelve months after entry into force, and Parliament and Council have yet to negotiate it — [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2548994); [EP Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act)

### Inferences
- Two workable legal constructions exist today: (a) "tool" model, where the agent's clicks are the consumer's own declaration (Computererklärung logic); (b) "representative" model, where the operating company holds a text-form Vollmacht and declares in the consumer's name. Model (b) is the one existing switching services use and is the safer basis for cancellations, because §174 and §312h BGB require a presentable, text-form authority.
- Under model (a) a "hallucinated" or mis-specified purchase is hard to undo by avoidance: the agent's poor choice is closer to a non-contestable motive error than to a §119/§120 error. In B2C distance sales the practical safety net is the 14-day withdrawal right, which does not cover all categories (e.g. dated travel services), so the operator's contractual liability to the user is the real backstop.
- Whether a contract concluded by an agent via API or a non-compliant page satisfies §312j is open: the provision assumes a human reading a button. If the trader's agent-facing checkout lacks a compliant confirmation step, the contract may not come into existence, which is a risk for sellers more than for consumers.
- Pre-contractual information duties are owed to the consumer, not the agent. An agent that ingests the information but does not pass it on in a durable medium probably leaves the trader non-compliant, with the consequence of extended withdrawal periods. A compliant design stores and forwards mandatory information to the user.
- The DFA's consumer-set approval thresholds would turn "human-in-the-loop by default" into law; building this now is a no-regret design choice.
- The cancellation and withdrawal buttons are enablers: they give an agent standardised, legally backed exit paths on every German consumer website.

### Gaps
- The DFA text itself was not retrieved. Article numbers, whether traders must accept agent-concluded contracts, liability allocation, and whether agent providers are regulated as "traders" are unverified.
- No 2025-2026 German court decision specifically on contract conclusion by an autonomous LLM agent was found.
- The ELI model rules on digital assistants for consumer contracts and the UNCITRAL Model Law on Automated Contracting (2024) are relevant soft law but were not retrieved.
- Whether the reported "eternal withdrawal right" cap for financial services is part of the June 2026 package was not confirmed.
- Travel (package travel law, §§651a ff. BGB) and sale-of-goods specifics were not researched.

## 2. Insurance distribution

### Takeaway
Comparing, recommending or concluding insurance for German consumers is licensed insurance distribution (§34d GewO, IDD) with advice, documentation and first-information duties that courts have already enforced against Check24. Whether a general-purpose chatbot that gives individual insurance recommendations is itself an intermediary is an open, live question raised with BaFin and DIHK in 2026 with no published answer found.

### Cited Findings
- IDD (Directive (EU) 2016/97) Art. 2(1)(1) defines insurance distribution to include providing information on insurance contracts according to criteria selected by customers through a website or other media, and providing a ranking list including price and product comparison, where the customer can directly or indirectly conclude a contract — [IDD](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/97/oj)
- Insurance intermediaries (broker or agent) need a licence from the Chamber of Industry and Commerce under §34d(1) GewO; fee-only insurance advisers are licensed under §34d(2) and may not take insurer remuneration — [§34d GewO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gewo/__34d.html); [IHK Frankfurt](https://www.frankfurt-main.ihk.de/standortpolitik/finanzplatz-frankfurt/finanzdienstleister-am-finanzplatz-frankfurt/brancheninformationen/versicherungsvermittlung-nach-34d-gewo)
- Brokers must ask about the customer's wishes and needs, advise, give reasons and document this (§61 VVG), hand over the documentation before conclusion (§62 VVG), and are liable for breach (§63 VVG); a broker must base advice on a sufficient number of contracts and insurers on the market (§60 VVG); the customer can waive advice only by separate declaration (§61(2) VVG) — [§60 VVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vvg_2008/__60.html); [§61 VVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vvg_2008/__61.html); [§63 VVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vvg_2008/__63.html)
- First-information duties (status, register number, remuneration type) are set out in §15 VersVermV — [VersVermV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/versvermv_2018/__15.html)
- Passing commission on to customers is prohibited (§48b VAG), with narrow exceptions — [§48b VAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vag_2016/__48b.html)
- Check24 litigation: LG München I (July 2016, 37 O 15268/15) and OLG München (April 2017, 29 U 2139/16), on the action of the broker association BVK, held that Check24 acts as an insurance broker, must actively provide the first information in text form (mere retrievability on the website is insufficient), and must meet advice and documentation standards online; Check24 accepted the OLG judgment in September 2017 — [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/lgmuencheni-37O1526815-check24-versicherungen-portal-online-makler-beratungspflichten); [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/recht/weitere-rechtsgebiete/allg-zivilrecht/check24-beratungs-und-dokumentationspflichten_208_417282.html); [Stiftung Warentest](https://www.test.de/Check24-Portal-weist-deutlicher-auf-Maklerstatus-hin-5046048-0/)
- The same litigation did not require commission disclosure at first contact — [Das Investment](https://www.dasinvestment.com/rechtsexperte-kommentiert-check24-urteil-keine-provisionsoffenlegung-beim-erstkontakt/)
- 2026: the intermediaries' association Votum observed that ChatGPT (OpenAI) and Claude (Anthropic) give individual insurance recommendations without a §34d licence and asked BaFin and DIHK for a regulatory classification, warning of a "liability vacuum"; BVK issued similar warnings and names EIOPA as relevant; trade press reported calls for EU-level regulation on 6 Jul 2026 — [cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/unerlaubte-ki-empfehlungen-votum-nimmt-chatgpt-und-claude-ins-visier-718600/); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4949908/Vermittlerverband-ruft-BaFin-und-DIHK-an-Ist-KI-Versicherungsberatung-erlaubnispflichtig/); [IWW, 6 Jul 2026](https://www.iww.de/vvp/vertreterrecht/versicherungsvermittlung-ki-chatbots-regulierungsbedarf-auf-eu-ebene-wegen-konkreter-versicherungsempfehlungen-f174871)
- Trade press reports that one in four customers uses AI research for insurance — [cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/versicherung-chatbot-jeder-vierte-kunde-nutzt-ki-recherche-730284/)
- AI Act market surveillance for intermediaries licensed under §§34d, 34f, 34h, 34i GewO sits with the Bundesnetzagentur under the German implementing act (KI-MIG), reported as in force since 29 Jul 2026 — [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4953138/Bundesnetzagentur-uebernimmt-KI-Marktueberwachung-fuer-Versicherungsvermittler/); [cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/ki-aufsicht-fuer-vermittler-bundesnetzagentur-zustaendig-730588/)
- Retail Investment Strategy (UNRESOLVED): political agreement was reached and the Council endorsed the final text in June 2026; it introduces a common inducements framework (new Art. 29a IDD) without an EU-wide commission ban, leaves Member States free to impose national bans, and adds value-for-money benchmarks set by EIOPA for insurance-based investment products; expected application about 24 months after entry into force (around end-2028) — [Linklaters](https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102n31i/eu-retail-investment-strategy-whats-in-the-final-rules); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/de/en/insights/corporate-governance-and-compliance/retail-investment-strategy-2026.html)
- One source reports that on 12 Nov 2026 the European Parliament rejected the provisionally agreed RIS Directive while approving the PRIIPs amending regulation — [Arthur Cox](https://www.arthurcox.com/insights/eu-retail-investment-strategy-parliament-rejects-directive-but-approves-priips-reforms/); earlier sources expected adoption in September or Q4 2026 — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/de/en/insights/corporate-governance-and-compliance/retail-investment-strategy-2026.html)

### Inferences
- An agent that ranks insurance offers by user criteria and lets the user conclude "directly or indirectly" falls squarely within the IDD definition. The operator needs a §34d licence (broker status is the natural fit for a multi-insurer agent), professional indemnity cover, and a compliant advice record per contract. This is a moat against unlicensed general-purpose assistants and a cost for any entrant.
- For a third-party model provider (e.g. OpenAI), the decisive IDD criterion is whether the customer can conclude through it and whether it is remunerated. A chatbot that only explains products is closer to exempt "mere information"; one with checkout or affiliate links moves towards distribution. No authority has ruled.
- The §61/§62 VVG documentation duty is something an LLM agent can fulfil well (automatic needs analysis and written reasons), turning a compliance burden into a product feature. A fully autonomous "silent switch" of insurance without a per-contract advice record or waiver is not compliant.
- The commission pass-on ban (§48b VAG) restricts cashback-style business models in insurance; a subscription-fee model raises the broker-versus-adviser question (§34d(1) vs (2) GewO).
- Insurance is excluded from the one-month termination rule of §309 Nr. 9 BGB and from the cancellation button where stricter form applies, so switching is tied to annual renewal dates and special termination rights.

### Gaps
- No BGH judgment on Check24's broker duties was found; the line of authority ends at OLG München 2017. Later Check24 cases (e.g. on guarantees, ratings, comparison completeness) were not researched.
- BaFin and EIOPA positions on robo-advice and AI governance were not retrieved this session; no BaFin/DIHK reply to Votum was found.
- The Parliament's reported 12 Nov 2026 rejection of the RIS Directive rests on a single source and needs verification.

## 3. Credit, banking and payments

### Takeaway
Credit comparison and brokering is licensed (§34c / §34i GewO) and, since 20 Nov 2026, governed by the CCD2 regime with human-review rights against automated credit decisions. Agent-initiated payments have no special regime: strong customer authentication still presumes a human, PSD3/PSR is agreed but not yet applicable, and FiDA is stalled.

### Cited Findings
- CCD2 (Directive (EU) 2023/2225) applies from 20 Nov 2026; it covers credit up to EUR 100,000 including buy-now-pay-later and small loans; Art. 18 requires a creditworthiness assessment and, where it involves automated processing, gives the consumer the right to human intervention, an explanation of the logic, to express their view and to request review; Art. 37 requires admission, registration and supervision of non-bank creditors and credit intermediaries — [Directive (EU) 2023/2225](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2225/oj); [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/de/insights/die-neue-verbraucherkreditrichtlinie-ccd-ii-was-marktteilnehmer-jetzt-wissen-und-bis)
- German transposition: the Bundestag passed the consumer credit act (reported 20 Apr 2026), the Bundesrat approved, and it enters into force on 20 Nov 2026; it brings BNPL into scope — [Otto Schmidt](https://www.otto-schmidt.de/news/wirtschaftsrecht/bundestag-beschliesst-gesetz-zu-verbraucherkrediten-2026-04-20.html); [Bundesregierung](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/suche/schutz-kreditvertraege-2382528); [CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/widerrufsbutton-auch-fuer-verbraucherkredite)
- Merchants offering credit face BaFin registration and new duties under the transposition — [CMS, CCD II: BaFin-Registrierung](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/ccd-ii-bafin-registrierung-und-neue-pflichten-fuer-haendler)
- The German rules do not oblige lenders to install a withdrawal button for consumer loans (CMS analysis) — [CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/widerrufsbutton-auch-fuer-verbraucherkredite)
- Loan brokers need a licence under §34c GewO; mortgage credit intermediaries under §34i GewO — [§34c GewO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gewo/__34c.html); [§34i GewO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gewo/__34i.html)
- CJEU, 7 Dec 2023, C-634/21 (SCHUFA): producing a credit score is itself an automated individual decision under Art. 22 GDPR where the lender draws strongly on it — [CJEU press release](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2023-12/cp230186pt.pdf); [vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/urteile/zur-scoring-praxis-der-schufa)
- CJEU, 27 Feb 2025, C-203/22 (Dun & Bradstreet Austria): the data subject is entitled to a meaningful explanation of the procedure and principles actually applied; trade secrets require a balancing by the court or authority — [heise](https://heise.de/-10299294); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/03/ecj-on-scoring)
- Payments today (IN FORCE): agent-based payments remain subject to PSD2 and the SCA technical standards with no separate regime; SCA is required where an agent initiates a payment or sets up a mandate for merchant-initiated transactions unless an exemption applies — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/agentic-payments-new-challenge-europes-payments-ecosystem); [Ashurst](https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/ai-powered-payment-agents-the-next-payments-revolution)
- Traditional SCA assumes a human entering a PIN, biometric or push approval; proposed workarounds are scope- and time-limited tokens attesting an earlier user authentication; liability for unauthorised agent-initiated transactions is a central open risk — [Freshfields, May 2026](https://www.freshfields.com/ja/our-thinking/briefings/2026/05/agentic-ai-in-the-payments-chain-regulatory-challenges-for-financial-institutions); [Ashurst](https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/ai-powered-payment-agents-the-next-payments-revolution)
- An EBA speech of 16 Oct 2026 says SCA reduced fraud but was not designed for current threats, and that EBA expects its PSR/PSD3 mandates around December 2026 or January 2027 — [EBA speech](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/76bb7c6b-29df-4211-b827-2e6b35225067/Payments%20Tomorrow%20Conference%20speech.pdf)
- PSD3/PSR (ADOPTED TEXT, NOT YET APPLICABLE): provisional agreement 27 Nov 2025; COREPER endorsement 22 Apr 2026; final compromise texts published 23 Apr 2026; ECON approval 5 May 2026; PSR to apply 21 months after entry into force (one source says 18), with later dates for some verification-of-payee provisions; banks must provide an integrated permissions dashboard — [Arthur Cox](https://www.arthurcox.com/insights/psd3-and-psr-final-compromise-texts-published/); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2026/04/impact-of-psd3-are-you-ready.pdf); [Open Banking Tracker](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/psd3-psr-readiness); [Worldline](https://worldline.com/en/home/main-navigation/resources/blogs/2026/the-scope-and-timeline-are-locked-in-for-psd3-and-psr-what-should-psps-know)
- FiDA (PROPOSAL, UNRESOLVED): trilogues remained open in 2026; the Council had not converged on whether to exclude DMA gatekeepers from data access even where licensed; Germany's position is described as pivotal — [Council working document WK 5041/2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/WK-5041-2026-INIT/en/pdf); [Projective Group, May 2026](https://www.projectivegroup.com/fida-back-in-motion-from-ambitious-to-workable/)
- Account switching: the Zahlungskontengesetz obliges banks to provide a switching service on the consumer's authorisation — [ZKG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zkg/)

### Inferences
- A credit-comparison agent needs §34c (and §34i for mortgages) licensing and must design for the CCD2 human-intervention right; fully automated credit placement with no human escalation path is non-compliant from 20 Nov 2026.
- For payments, the realistic 2026-2027 architecture is user-authenticated mandates (SEPA direct debit to the supplier, card-on-file with merchant-initiated transactions, or per-transaction SCA step-up). The agent should not hold raw banking credentials.
- Open-finance data access beyond payment accounts (insurance, pensions, loans) cannot be relied on before roughly 2029 given FiDA's state; PSD2 account-information access (as a licensed or partnered AISP) is the only statutory data pipe today. A FiDA gatekeeper exclusion, if adopted, would be a moat for non-gatekeeper agents.

### Gaps
- Whether PSD3/PSR had been published in the Official Journal by 23 Nov 2026 could not be confirmed; no source showed publication.
- No EBA opinion or Q&A specifically on SCA for AI agents was found.
- Exact wording of the German CCD2 transposition (new §§505a ff. BGB, §34c GewO changes, credit intermediary registration) was not retrieved.
- A German statutory scoring rule (often discussed as §37a BDSG) was searched and not found.

## 4. Energy

### Takeaway
Energy is the most agent-friendly vertical: the supplier switch is technically completed within 24 hours on working days since 6 Jun 2025, the new supplier or a switching service acts on a text-form Vollmacht, and contract-term law limits lock-in. Switching services such as Wechselpilot and SwitchUp rest on ordinary agency law, not on a sector licence.

### Cited Findings
- Since 6 Jun 2025 (postponed from 4 Apr 2025) electricity supplier switches must be technically possible within 24 hours on working days under the Bundesnetzagentur determination BK6-22-024; gas is not covered; retroactive registration and deregistration are no longer possible; contractual notice periods are unaffected — [Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Vportal/Energie/KuendigungLieferantenwechsel/Lieferantenwechsel/start.html); [BK6-22-024 notice](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Beschlusskammern/1_GZ/BK6-GZ/2022/BK6-22-024/Mitteilung_Nr_04/Mitteilung_Nr_04.html); [Vattenfall](https://www.vattenfall.de/geschaeftskunden/vrees/lieferantenwechsel-24-stunden)
- The new supplier normally terminates the old contract and needs the customer's Vollmacht; providing the 11-digit market location ID enables the fast switch — [Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Vportal/Energie/KuendigungLieferantenwechsel/Lieferantenwechsel/start.html); [EnBW](https://www.enbw.com/service/faq/24h-lieferantenwechsel)
- The authority to terminate must be in Textform (§312h BGB); LG München I held that the new supplier must hold it even though the market process does not check it; unauthorised terminations by competitors were treated as unfair obstruction (LG München I, 8 Nov 2021, 33 O 480/21) — [vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/pressemitteilungen/finger-weg-von-meinem-stromvertrag-ohne-vollmacht-keine-kuendigung); [LG München I 33 O 480/21](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Rtf/Y-300-Z-GRURRS-B-2021-N-35995?all=False)
- Stiftung Warentest: a tariff change by a switching service requires the customer's permission; it tested nine switching services including SwitchUp — [Stiftung Warentest](https://www.test.de/Strom-Tarifwechsel-nur-mit-Erlaubnis-des-Kunden-5471642-0/)
- Supply contracts with household customers outside basic supply, and their termination, require Textform; suppliers must offer dynamic tariffs to customers with smart metering — [§41b EnWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/enwg_2005/__41b.html); [§41a EnWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/enwg_2005/__41a.html)
- Switching rules are anchored in §20a EnWG — [§20a EnWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/enwg_2005/__20a.html)
- Bundeskartellamt sector inquiry (final report April 2019): energy comparison rankings were influenced by commission, non-paying suppliers mostly did not appear, and "position 0" placements were shown ahead of the ranking — [Bundeskartellamt](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/EN/Consumer_protection/Protecting_Consumer_Rights/ComparisonWebsites/ComparisonWebsites.html); [Bundeskartellamt press release, 11 Apr 2019](https://bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2019/11_04_2019_Vergleichsportale.html?nn=48916)

### Inferences
- The legal basis for Wechselpilot/SwitchUp-style services is a civil-law mandate plus text-form Vollmacht (§§164 ff., 312h BGB) to conclude the new contract and terminate the old one; no energy-specific licence was identified. An AI agent run by a company can use the same construction.
- Combined with one-month termination after the initial term (§309 Nr. 9 BGB), the 24-hour process makes continuous optimisation feasible; first-year bonuses and price guarantees still tie optimisation to contract anniversaries.
- An agent that includes non-commission-paying suppliers would differ from the practices the Bundeskartellamt criticised, but must then disclose ranking parameters and any payments (see section 8).

### Gaps
- No court decision or regulator statement specifically on Wechselpilot or SwitchUp was found, nor on suppliers refusing switching-service authorities.
- Rules on bonuses and price guarantees (case law on bonus forfeiture, price-change notices under §41(5) EnWG) were not retrieved.
- The 2025-2026 EnWG amendments (consumer-protection changes, energy sharing) were not researched.

## 5. Telecoms

### Takeaway
The TKG 2021 caps initial terms at 24 months, allows monthly cancellation after the minimum term, and guarantees number porting and a managed provider switch, so telecoms is structurally switchable but only at the end of the initial term.

### Cited Findings
- §56 TKG: initial term at most 24 months; after tacit renewal the customer may terminate at any time with one month's notice; no charges may arise from that termination — [§56 TKG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tkg_2021/__56.html); [Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/aktuelle-meldungen/digitale-welt/telefon-handy-und-internetvertraege-wichtige-neue-kundenrechte-65879)
- §54 TKG: a contract summary must be provided before conclusion; §59 TKG regulates the provider-switch process and number porting including compensation for delays; §57 TKG gives reduction and termination rights for under-performance — [§54 TKG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tkg_2021/__54.html); [§59 TKG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tkg_2021/__59.html); [§57 TKG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tkg_2021/__57.html)
- The cancellation button (§312k BGB) covers telecoms contracts concluded online — [IHK Osnabrück](https://www.ihk.de/osnabrueck/recht-und-fair-play/recht/internetrecht/kuendigungsbutton-5612382)
- From 24 Dec 2027 (reported date), telecoms providers are among the private relying parties that must accept the EUDI Wallet where strong user authentication is required — [Heuking, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.heuking.de/de/news-events/newsletter-fachbeitraege/artikel/digitale-identitaet-mit-annahmepflicht-eudi-wallet-und-european-business-wallet-im-ueberblick.html)

### Inferences
- The switch is led by the receiving provider, so an agent acting on a text-form Vollmacht (§312h BGB) can order the new contract and trigger porting; identity checks for prepaid/SIM activation remain a human step until wallet-based identification is available.
- The pre-contract summary must reach the consumer; if the agent "reads" it, the agent should archive and forward it.

### Gaps
- Exact porting deadlines and compensation amounts under §59 TKG were not re-verified this session.
- No sources on telecoms providers' handling of third-party authorities or agent-driven switching were found.

## 6. Data protection

### Takeaway
An always-on agent holding contract and financial data can rest on contract performance and consent, but needs a DPIA, strict purpose limitation and care that its own choices do not become solely automated decisions with legal effect under Art. 22 GDPR. The Digital Omnibus changes to the GDPR are still in the legislative process.

### Cited Findings
- Legal bases, purpose limitation, data portability (Art. 20), automated decisions (Art. 22) and DPIA (Art. 35) are governed by the GDPR — [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- CJEU SCHUFA (C-634/21, 7 Dec 2023): Art. 22 applies to a score that decisively shapes a third party's decision; Member State exceptions under Art. 22(2)(b) are limited — [CJEU press release](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2023-12/cp230186pt.pdf)
- CJEU C-203/22 (27 Feb 2025): Art. 15(1)(h) requires a meaningful, intelligible explanation of the logic involved — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/03/ecj-on-scoring)
- Storing or reading information on the user's device requires consent unless strictly necessary (§25 TDDDG) — [§25 TDDDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ttdsg/__25.html)
- Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) gives users access and third-party sharing rights for connected-product data, with gatekeepers excluded as recipients — [Data Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj)
- Digital Omnibus, data part (PROPOSAL): Commission proposal of November 2025 amends GDPR, ePrivacy, NIS2 and Data Act; the Council agreed its negotiating position on 21 Oct 2026, keeping the definition of personal data and adding a new Art. 25a on pseudonymised data; the Parliament's position is outstanding; observers expect talks to run beyond February 2027 — [Lewis Silkin, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/11/09/digital-rulebook-simplification-of-eu-data-protection-and-cyber-rules-102o7e4); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/eu-member-states-finalize-digital-omnibus-negotiating-stance); [IAPP on delays](https://iapp.org/news/a/one-year-on-digital-omnibus-may-face-delays); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/global/intellectual-property/art/digital-omnibus-eu-council-establishes-position)
- EDRi criticises the Council position as weakening data-protection rights — [EDRi](https://edri.org/?p=63792)

### Inferences
- Art. 6(1)(b) covers processing needed to deliver the agent service; health data in insurance applications needs explicit consent (Art. 9). Reusing contract data for advertising or for commission optimisation needs a separate basis.
- Art. 22 cuts both ways: when the agent decides for the user at the user's instruction, the user is exercising autonomy, but a provider-side refusal driven by the agent's or a scorer's profile is an Art. 22 decision. Consumer-set approval thresholds and a human escalation path reduce the risk.
- Art. 20 portability and Art. 15 access are levers for an agent to extract the user's contract data from incumbents, but there is no real-time API duty outside PSD2; a text-form authority to exercise data-subject rights on the user's behalf should be part of onboarding.
- A DPIA is practically unavoidable (large-scale profiling of financial circumstances, new technology).

### Gaps
- No data-protection authority guidance (EDPB, DSK, BfDI) specific to AI agents acting for consumers was retrieved.
- The Digital Omnibus provisions relevant to automated decisions and AI training were not verified in the Council text.

## 7. AI Act and AI liability

### Takeaway
A comparison-and-switching agent is mainly a limited-risk system with Art. 50 transparency duties that apply since 2 Aug 2026; it becomes high-risk only if it performs creditworthiness evaluation or life/health insurance risk assessment and pricing, and those obligations were postponed to 2 Dec 2027.

### Cited Findings
- AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689): Annex III point 5(b) lists creditworthiness evaluation and credit scoring of natural persons, and point 5(c) risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance, as high-risk; Art. 50(1) requires providers to ensure people are informed they are interacting with an AI system — [AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- Art. 50 has applied since 2 Aug 2026; the chatbot disclosure duty falls on providers, with separate deployer duties; providers of systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026 have until 2 Dec 2026 for machine-readable marking under Art. 50(2) — [IHK Hannover](https://www.ihk.de/hannover/hauptnavigation/digitalisierung/aktuell-digitalisierung/ai-act-neue-transparenzpflichten-gelten-ab-dem-2-august-7129608); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august); [CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/deepfakes-chatbots-co.-die-neuen-transparenzpflichten-der-ki-vo)
- Digital Omnibus on AI (IN FORCE): published 24 Jul 2026 as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026; stand-alone Annex III high-risk obligations moved from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027, product-embedded high-risk to 2 Aug 2028; EU database registration retained — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/spain/intellectual-property/art/digital-omnibus-ai-has-been-published); [Lewis Silkin, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/27/the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force-today-102nedo); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/05/the-eu-digital-omnibus-on-ai-what-the-political-deal-means)
- German supervision: Bundesnetzagentur is the default market surveillance authority under the KI-MIG, reported in force since 29 Jul 2026 — [cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/ki-aufsicht-fuer-vermittler-bundesnetzagentur-zustaendig-730588/)
- A 2026 academic paper argues agentic payments should be expressly recognised as a high-risk use case under the AI Act — [Cambridge University Press (European Journal of Risk Regulation)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S1867299X26101032)
- Liability: AI Liability Directive withdrawn (2025); new Product Liability Directive applies to products placed on the market after 9 Dec 2026 and covers software and AI — [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/proposed-eu-ai-liability-rules-withdrawn); [IHK Köln](https://www.ihk.de/koeln/hauptnavigation/recht-steuern/neues-produkthaftungsrecht-ab-dezember-2026-7219976)

### Inferences
- An agent that only collects offers and passes user data to lenders or insurers who do their own scoring stays outside Annex III; pre-screening users for credit eligibility or pricing life/health cover itself would bring the operator into high-risk territory from 2 Dec 2027.
- An operator building on a third-party foundation model is a "provider" of its own AI system (the agent) and a downstream user of a general-purpose model whose provider carries the GPAI duties (applicable since Aug 2025).
- Product liability is strict but covers death, injury, property damage and data loss, not pure financial loss; a bad tariff choice is therefore a contract-law and professional-liability matter (e.g. §63 VVG), not a product-liability one.

### Gaps
- No Commission guidance classifying consumer purchasing agents under the AI Act was found.
- The final text of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was not read; other changes (e.g. SME relief, AI literacy) are unverified.

## 8. Competition and platform law

### Takeaway
Ranking transparency and paid-placement disclosure duties already bind any agent that ranks offers and takes commission. Gatekeeper law is moving towards AI assistants: Google received binding DMA measures on AI interoperability and search-data sharing in July 2026, and ChatGPT is under gatekeeper assessment but not designated. Sellers' ability to block agents is unsettled in the EU.

### Cited Findings
Ranking and commission transparency (IN FORCE)
- §5b(2) UWG: anyone offering consumers a search across offers from different traders must disclose the main ranking parameters and their relative weighting; hidden paid placement in search results is a blacklisted practice (Annex to §3(3) UWG, no. 11a) — [§5b UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__5b.html); [UWG Annex](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/anhang.html)
- P2B Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 Art. 5 requires online intermediation services to disclose main ranking parameters and any influence of remuneration to business users — [P2B Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/1150/oj)
- DSA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065): Art. 25 bans deceptive interface design on online platforms, Art. 27 requires recommender-system transparency, Art. 30 trader traceability on marketplaces — [DSA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj)
- Bundeskartellamt sector inquiry into comparison portals (opened Oct 2017, final report April 2019): rankings depend partly on fees and commission, non-paying providers are mostly not shown, hotel and energy rankings are influenced by commission level, "position 0" placements precede the ranking; conduct "does not match the ideal of a neutral platform" — [Bundeskartellamt](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/EN/Consumer_protection/Protecting_Consumer_Rights/ComparisonWebsites/ComparisonWebsites.html); [Bundeskartellamt press release](https://bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2019/11_04_2019_Vergleichsportale.html?nn=48916); [vzbv statement](https://www.vzbv.de/sites/default/files/downloads/2019/02/04/19-02-04_vzbv_stn_vergleichsportale.pdf)

Gatekeepers and AI assistants
- DMA (Regulation (EU) 2022/1925) lists "virtual assistants" as a core platform service and bans self-preferencing in ranking (Art. 6(5)) — [DMA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/1925/oj)
- ChatGPT: designated a very large online search engine under the DSA in 2026; on 30 Jul 2026 a Commission spokesperson said ChatGPT had reported user numbers above DMA thresholds and designation is possible; no designation decision was found as of 23 Nov 2026 — [TechPolicy.Press](https://techpolicy.press/what-chatgpts-dsa-designation-means-for-openai-and-the-eu); [eunews, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.eunews.it/en/2026/07/30/chatgpt-and-roblox-in-the-eus-sights-as-brussels-prepares-to-trigger-the-digital-markets-act/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/hatgpt-reddit-roblox-dsa-designation)
- Commentary: AI services can only be designated if they fit an existing core-platform-service category (search engine, browser, virtual assistant); the first DMA review prefers evidence-gathering over major change — [Hausfeld](https://www.hausfeld.com/what-we-think/competition-bulletin/regulating-artificial-intelligence-between-the-eu-digital-markets-act-and-competition-law); [TechPolicy.Press on DMA review](https://techpolicy.press/what-the-eus-first-digital-markets-act-review-actually-changes); [Commission DMA review Q&A](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/about-dma/dma-review-qa_en)
- Google: on 16 Jul 2026 the Commission adopted two binding specification decisions: third-party AI assistants get effective access to 11 Android features (benefits expected from July 2027), and Google must share anonymised search data with rival search engines including AI search engines from January 2027 — [European Commission](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/commission-provides-guidance-google-ai-interoperability-android-and-sharing-google-search-data-under-2026-07-16_en); [Commission Q&A on Android AI interoperability](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/developer-portal/interoperability/alphabet-specification-proceedings-interoperability-ai-services_en); [Kluwer Competition Law Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/competition-blog/keeping-the-gate-the-european-commission-issues-its-specification-decision-on-alphabets-operating-system-interoperability-with-ai-assistants/)
- Meta: on 9 Jun 2026 the Commission ordered Meta under Art. 102 TFEU to restore free WhatsApp access for rival general-purpose AI assistants (as reported) — [Hausfeld](https://www.hausfeld.com/what-we-think/perspectives-blogs/reaching-tipping-point)
- Google Shopping: the CJEU upheld the EUR 2.42 billion fine for self-preferencing of Google's own comparison shopping service (C-48/22 P, 10 Sep 2024) — [CJEU C-48/22 P](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62022CJ0048)
- Germany: the Bundeskartellamt has completed five §19a GWB proceedings with final effect since 2021; a draft 12th GWB amendment was published 4 Jun 2026, and an expert in the October 2026 Bundestag hearing proposed supplementing §19a with AI rules; the authority's 2025/26 report addresses AI data access, compute and cooperations — [BT-Drs. 21/5449](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/054/2105449.pdf); [Noerr on 12th GWB amendment](https://www.noerr.com/de/insights/12-gwb-novelle-uberblick-uber-den-referentenentwurf-des-bmwe-vom-04062026); [Podszun statement](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/1224414/Stellungnahme_SV_Dr-_Rupprecht_Podszun_HH-Uni_Duesseldorf.pdf); [Rödl](https://www.roedl.com/insights/jahresbericht-2025-26-bundeskartellamt/)

Blocking agents and scraping
- BGH, 30 Apr 2014, I ZR 224/12 (Ryanair/flight portal): screen scraping against the operator's stated will is not in itself unfair obstruction; the result could differ if technical protection measures are circumvented, the site is measurably slowed, or the display misleads — [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/hintergruende/h/bgh-urteil-izr22412-screen-scraping-flugdaten-automatisiert-auslesen-ryanair-reiseportal); [RA Plutte](https://www.ra-plutte.de/bgh-zum-automatisierten-auslesen-fremder-websites-via-screen-scraping/)
- CJEU, 15 Jan 2015, C-30/14 (Ryanair v PR Aviation): where a database is protected neither by copyright nor by the sui generis right, the Database Directive does not limit contractual restrictions on its use, so terms of use can prohibit scraping — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/de/insights/besser-keinen-gesetzlichen-datenbankschutz-eugh-lasst-vertragliche-beschrankungen-von-datenbanknutzung-zu-wenn-die-datenbank-nicht-gesetzlich-geschutzt-ist); [EU Law Radar](https://eulawradar.com/case-c-3014-ryanair-grounding-a-go-compare-an-airfare-website/)
- CJEU, 3 Jun 2021, C-762/19 (CV-Online Latvia): sui generis database protection against a specialised search engine depends on harm to the maker's investment — [CJEU C-762/19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62019CJ0762)
- United States (not binding in the EU): a district court enjoined Perplexity's Comet agent from accessing password-protected parts of Amazon in March 2026; the Ninth Circuit reversed on 4 Aug 2026, reasoning that when a user directs the assistant it is the user who accesses Amazon; the underlying case continues — [Bloomberg, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-10/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexity-s-ai-shopping-bots); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/amazon-loses-perplexity-comet-ai-shopping-ruling); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/perplexity-comet-amazon-ai-shopping-agents-ruling)
- A CERRE paper examines whether the DMA is ready for agentic AI — [CERRE](https://cerre.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Is-the-DMA-Ready-for-Agentic-AI_Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- An agent that returns a ranked shortlist is a "search across offers" under §5b(2) UWG: ranking parameters and any commission influence must be disclosed, and commission-driven "top picks" need labelling. A single-answer agent ("I chose X for you") makes hidden commission bias a higher misleading-practice risk, not a lower one.
- If a gatekeeper-designated assistant steers users to its own or partner commerce services, Art. 6(5) DMA and the Google Shopping precedent become the specialist agent's main legal weapon; until ChatGPT is designated, only Art. 102 TFEU and §19a GWB (for already designated firms) are available.
- The Android interoperability decision is an enabler for independent agents on mobile from mid-2027.
- EU law gives sellers more room than the Ninth Circuit's reasoning suggests: terms-of-use bans can bind where no database right applies (C-30/14), and a logged-in agent acts under the user's account contract. Access through official APIs, partner agreements or the user's own data rights is more durable than scraping. Dominant sellers that block agents face abuse-of-dominance arguments.

### Gaps
- No German decision or commentary on "virtuelles Hausrecht" applied to AI agents was found.
- No §19a GWB proceeding concerning OpenAI or AI assistants was found.
- The legal basis and date of ChatGPT's DSA designation were not verified from a Commission source.
- Whether the 12th GWB amendment gives the Bundeskartellamt consumer-law enforcement powers was not verified.

## 9. Identity, e-signature and delegation (eIDAS 2.0, EUDI Wallet)

### Takeaway
Germany's state EUDI Wallet is due to launch with a basic offer at the start of January 2027, with mandatory acceptance by banks and other regulated private sectors from late December 2027. It will ease identification and signing, but no official credential for delegating authority to an AI agent exists yet.

### Cited Findings
- eIDAS 2.0 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1183): each Member State must provide at least one wallet; private relying parties that must use strong user authentication (including banking, financial services, energy, telecoms) and very large online platforms must accept the wallet; natural persons get free qualified electronic signatures for non-professional use — [Regulation (EU) 2024/1183](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1183/oj)
- Germany: the state wallet is reported under the name "d-you", commissioned by the digital ministry and developed by SPRIND, launching with a basic offer at the start of 2027 (one article names 2 Jan 2027) and adding credentials step by step; some eIDAS functions are missing at launch; use cases named include age proof, contract signing and account opening — [Heuking, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.heuking.de/de/news-events/newsletter-fachbeitraege/artikel/digitale-identitaet-mit-annahmepflicht-eudi-wallet-und-european-business-wallet-im-ueberblick.html); [Börsen-Zeitung](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/finanzen-technik/die-digitale-brieftasche-eudi-wallet-startet-halb-gefuellt); [PC-Welt](https://www.pcwelt.de/?p=3232008)
- Banks that must use strong authentication for online identification must accept the wallet from 24 Dec 2027 on customer request; public authorities from launch; industry flags open questions on the scope of the duty, its relation to payments law and missing API specifications — [Börsen-Zeitung](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/finanzen-technik/die-digitale-brieftasche-eudi-wallet-startet-halb-gefuellt); [IT-Finanzmagazin, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.it-finanzmagazin.de/?p=250547)
- Savings banks state they are launch partners of the state wallet — [IT-Finanzmagazin, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.it-finanzmagazin.de/?p=248302)
- ENISA published a draft wallet certification scheme in April 2026 — [Cloud Security Alliance research note (unofficial)](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-enisa-eu-digital-wallet-ai-agent-identity/)
- Delegation to agents: an ITU workshop presentation lists a "digital power of attorney" as missing; the WE BUILD pilot consortium recommends an EU strategy for safe AI agents built on the EUDI and Business Wallet frameworks; vendors describe the proposed European Business Wallet as a place to manage mandates; Council documents on the Business Wallet say it is not intended to affect powers of attorney under national law — [ITU workshop slides](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-T/Workshops-and-Seminars/2026/0330/Documents/Zoltan%20Pr%c3%a9csenyi.pdf); [Biometric Update, Mar 2026](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202603/eu-can-rein-in-ai-agents-with-eudi-wallets-and-business-wallets-we-build); [Namirial](https://www.namirial.com/en/blog/ecosystem/wallets-agentic-ai-trust/); [Council document ST 9684/2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-9684-2026-INIT/en/pdf)
- Customer due diligence: the EU AML Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 applies from 10 Jul 2027 and recognises electronic identification means under eIDAS for identity verification — [Regulation (EU) 2024/1624](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1624/oj)

### Inferences
- Until the wallet is widely accepted (2027-2028), identification for bank accounts, credit and SIM activation stays a human step (video or eID identification), which limits full autonomy in banking and telecoms.
- Free qualified signatures in the wallet will remove the written-form barrier for the few consumer contracts and waivers that need it, and give agents a strong way to obtain provable user approval for high-value actions.
- A verifiable, scoped, revocable "mandate credential" for agents is a gap in the framework; an operator can build a proprietary text-form Vollmacht now and migrate to wallet attestations later.

### Gaps
- No official EU or German specification for agent-delegation attestations in the EUDI Wallet was found; material is vendor, consortium or unofficial research.
- The exact legal launch deadline for Member State wallets and the precise German launch date need confirmation from an official source.

## 10. Rechtsdienstleistungsgesetz (RDG) limits

### Takeaway
Sending cancellations and switching contracts on a Vollmacht is ordinary agency, but disputing bills or enforcing claims requires individual legal assessment and is reserved to lawyers or registered collection service providers; the BGH has read the collection licence broadly for legal-tech models and has cleared document generators.

### Cited Findings
- A legal service is any activity in a specific matter of another person that requires a legal assessment of the individual case (§2(1) RDG); it needs statutory permission (§3 RDG); ancillary legal services connected to another main activity are allowed (§5 RDG); collection services need registration (§10 RDG) — [§2 RDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/rdg/__2.html); [§5 RDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/rdg/__5.html); [§10 RDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/rdg/__10.html)
- BGH, 27 Nov 2019, VIII ZR 285/18 (wenigermiete.de / LexFox, later Conny): the model is a legal service but is (still) covered by the registered collection licence under §10(1) no. 1 RDG, read broadly; assignment of the tenant's claims is valid — [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/juristen/b/bgh-viii-zr-285-18-legal-tech-wenigermiete-de-inkassodienstleistung-weite-auslegung-abtretung-wirksam-rechtsdienstleistungsgesetz); [BGH VIII ZR 285/18](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/bjq-27-11-2019-viii-zr-28518/)
- BGH, 30 Mar 2022, VIII ZR 256/21 confirmed and strengthened this line — [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/juristen/b/bgh-legal-tech-wenigermiete-zr25621-rechtsdienstleistung-inkasso-lexfox)
- BGH, 9 Sep 2021, I ZR 113/20 (Smartlaw): a software contract generator is not a legal service because the provider does not act in a specific matter of another; it is comparable to a form book — [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/juristen/b/bgh-urteil-izr11320-vertragsgenerator-smartlaw-keine-unzulaessige-rechtsdienstleistung-rak-hamburg-rdg-legal-tech); [beck-aktuell](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/heute-im-recht/rechtsprechung/bgh-keine-konkrete-taetigkeit-smartlaw-ist-zulaessig-2021-10-01)
- CJEU, 30 May 2024, C-400/22 arose from Conny's model and concerned the order-button duty, not the RDG — [CJEU C-400/22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/DE/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62022CJ0400)

### Inferences
- Cancelling at the user's instruction, switching suppliers and sending standard withdrawal declarations involve no individual legal assessment and stay outside §2(1) RDG. Assessing whether a price increase is invalid, contesting a bill, asserting a special termination right on disputed grounds or negotiating a settlement is a legal service.
- Three compliant routes exist for claims work: registration as a collection service provider with assignment or collection mandate (the Conny/Flightright structure), treating it as an ancillary service to brokerage under §5 RDG (plausible for an insurance broker's claims support, narrow elsewhere), or routing to partner lawyers. The Smartlaw logic covers self-service letter generators where the user acts in their own matter, but an autonomous agent that decides and sends on its own initiative is closer to acting in another's matter.
- An LLM agent that reasons about the individual case is harder to fit under Smartlaw than a rule-based generator; this is untested.

### Gaps
- No court decision on Aboalarm under the RDG was found; reported Aboalarm disputes concern validity and receipt of cancellations, which was not verified this session.
- No BGH decision specifically on Flightright's model under the RDG was retrieved.
- No case law on LLM-based legal assessment under the RDG was found.
