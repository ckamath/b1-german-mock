## 1. Business-model theory: what happens to commission/CPA models when the buyer is an agent?

### Takeaway
The best current economics (Shahidi et al., NBER 2025) says agents cut search, communication and contracting costs but create new frictions (congestion, obfuscation) and that the net welfare effect is still an empirical question; nothing in the evidence shows commissions disappearing, but lab studies show agents penalise "sponsored" labels, reward platform endorsements and concentrate demand on a few offers, which undermines paid-placement revenue and raises the value of being the trusted ranking/endorsement source. In Germany the "inertia rent" that funds today's commissions is large and measurable (22% of household electricity volume still in Grundversorgung in 2024; Verivox estimates EUR 5.4bn energy overpayment in H1 2026 alone).

### Cited Findings
- "The Coasean Singularity? Demand, Supply, and Market Design with AI Agents" (Shahidi, Rusak, Manning, Fradkin, Horton; NBER WP 34468, 2025; chapter in "The Economics of Transformative AI"): agent adoption is derived demand (users trade decision quality against effort saved); on the supply side outcomes depend on whether agents operate within one platform or across platforms; agents lower search, communication and contracting costs but introduce congestion and price obfuscation; they lower the cost of preference elicitation, contract enforcement and identity verification, widening the set of feasible market designs; net welfare effects "remain an empirical question". (abstract-level only) — [NBER WP 34468](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34468); [NBER chapter](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/economics-transformative-ai/coasean-singularity-demand-supply-and-market-design-ai-agents)
- Lab evidence that agents do not behave like ad-susceptible humans but have their own exploitable biases ("What Is Your AI Agent Buying?", Allouah, Besbes, Figueroa, Kanoria, Kumar; arXiv 2508.02630, v1 Aug 2025, v3 Dec 2025): shopping agents show strong, model-specific position bias; they penalise sponsored tags and reward platform endorsements; they concentrate demand on a few "modal" products; model updates can drastically reshuffle market shares; a seller-side agent making minor description tweaks can win substantial share. — [arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630v3)
- Microsoft Research "Magentic Marketplace" (preprint Oct 2025; simulation, not field data): all tested models showed severe first-proposal bias (10-30x advantage for response speed over quality; ~80-100% of agents accepted the first proposal in the blog summary); consumer welfare fell as the number of search results grew; most models contacted only a small fraction of available businesses; frontier models reached near-optimal welfare only when search quality was high; models were variably vulnerable to fake credentials, fake reviews and prompt injection. — [arXiv 2510.25779](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25779v1); [The Register, 6 Nov 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/06/microsoft_unveils_the_magentic_marketplace)
- Gartner "machine customers/custobots": CEOs on average expect 15-20% of revenue from machine customers by 2030; a 2025 Gartner prediction says 90% of B2B buying will be agent-intermediated by 2028 (>USD 15tn of B2B spend). These are forecasts/surveys, not outcomes. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/publications/when-machines-become-customers); [Digital Commerce 360, 28 Nov 2025](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/11/28/gartner-ai-agents-15-trillion-in-b2b-purchases-by-2028/amp/)
- Price parity (MFN) in Germany: the Bundeskartellamt opened proceedings against Check24 in July 2025 over clauses that stopped energy suppliers offering cheaper tariffs on other portals or in their own channel; on 24 Feb 2026 Check24's commitments were made binding (no fine; Check24 denies an infringement). Check24 committed that neither the contractual relationship nor the commission level will depend on the supplier's pricing in other channels. — [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/kanzleien-unternehmen/k/check24-gibt-preisparitaetsklauseln-nach-ermittlungen-des-bkarta-auf); [Rödl, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.roedl.com/insights/check24-bestpreisklauseln-bundeskartellamt/)
- Check24's energy channel power: per a "Vertriebskanalstudie 2025" cited by ZfK, 60-70% of (portal) energy contract conclusions run via Check24. — [ZfK](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/vertrieb/macht-der-portale-check24-verivox-preisvergleich-energie)
- Provider-side critique of the commission model ("Wechselautomat"): HUK-Coburg left all comparison portals from Oct 2017, citing cost; HUK's stated objection is that portals earn a fresh acquisition commission every time a customer shops for a new motor tariff; a press report cites EUR 70-100 per concluded contract paid to Check24 by cooperating insurers (old figure, product scope unclear). Allianz also ceased paying Check24 commission to cut distribution cost. — [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4879665/chapter/1/Check24-unterliegt-HUK-vor-dem-OLG-Koln/); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4858949/Huk-Coburg-Vergleichsportal-Ausstieg-teuer/); [Autohaus](https://www.autohaus.de/nachrichten/kfz-versicherer-huk-coburg-kehrt-vergleichsportalen-den-ruecken-2018446.html)
- Bundeskartellamt sector inquiry on comparison portals: portals are fundamentally pro-competitive, but some practices do not match the ideal of a neutral platform. — [CIO.de](https://www.cio.de/article/3683544/bundeskartellamt-ueberprueft-vergleichsportale.html)
- UK CMA Digital Comparison Tools market study: final report published 26 Sep 2017 (349 pages; Kantar survey of 4,083 consumers). (Detailed findings not retrieved, see Gaps.) — [CMA case page](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/digital-comparison-tools-market-study); [Final report PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/59c93546e5274a77468120d6/digital-comparison-tools-market-study-final-report.pdf)
- Loyalty penalty, UK regulatory precedent: FCA GIPP rules (ban on price walking, in force Jan 2022) were forecast at policy stage to save consumers GBP 4.2bn over 10 years. The FCA's July 2025 evaluation (EP25/2) found price walking was effectively reduced; the home new-vs-renewal price gap almost halved (GBP 95.38 to GBP 49.17); motor prices fell an estimated GBP 6.63 per policy attributable to the remedy; no statistically significant causal price effect in home; motor compulsory excesses rose; compliance costs exceeded forecasts. — [FCA EP25/2](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/corporate-documents/evaluation-paper-25-2-general-insurance-pricing-practices-remedies); [FCA press release on GIPP](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-confirms-measures-protect-customers-loyalty-penalty-home-motor-insurance-markets/printable/print)
- Size of the German inertia rent, energy: Monitoringbericht 2025 (published 26 Nov 2025, data year 2024): Grundversorgung share ~22% for electricity (prior year 25%) and ~16% for gas (prior 19%), per ZfK measured on volume sold; ~7.1m electricity and 2.2m gas customers switched supplier in 2024; a July 2025 pre-release counted >10m electricity supplier and contract changes combined (2023: 6m). — [BNetzA press release 26 Nov 2025](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2025/20251126_Monitoringbericht.html); [BNetzA 14 Jul 2025](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2025/20250714_MB2025.html); [ZfK](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/vertrieb/mehr-wechsel-weniger-grundversorgung-das-wichtigste-aus-dem-monitoringbericht-im-ueberblick)
- Verivox (portal's own estimate, 2026): German households overpaid ~EUR 5.4bn for energy in H1 2026; a house with 4,000 kWh electricity and 20,000 kWh gas could save EUR 1,315/year by leaving Grundversorgung (EUR 622 electricity + EUR 693 gas). — [Verivox](https://www.verivox.de/strom-gas/nachrichten/54-milliarden-euro-verschenkt-deutsche-haushalte-zahlen-zu-viel-fuer-strom-und-gas-1121588/)
- German motor insurance: ~1.7m contracts changed insurer in the 2025 year-end season (Sirius Campus survey, n=2,048, 1-11 Dec 2025); Destatis reported motor insurance prices +10.9% y/y in Sep 2025 vs 2.4% CPI; Verivox says a cheap tariff saves on average ~30% / EUR 180. — [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4947926/Kfz-Versicherung-Wechselaktivitaet-und-Kundenverhalten/); [Verivox Sparpotenziale](https://www.verivox.de/sparpotenziale-familie/)
- Early German evidence that AI is displacing the portal at the top of the funnel in insurance (trade press, 2026): headlines "KI: Vergleichsportale bekommen ein Problem" and "Verbraucher fragen zuerst die KI, dann erst Google"; one survey reported by Versicherungsbote found 16% of AI users find a suitable motor policy directly via AI and a further 23% feel informed enough to conclude directly. — [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4951473/KI-Vergleichsportale-bekommen-ein-Problem/); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4948671/Kuenstliche-Intelligenz-veraendert-Tarifsuche-in-der-Kfz-Versicherung/); [Cash-Online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/verbraucher-fragen-zuerst-die-ki-dann-erst-google-722715/)

### Inferences
- Commission/CPA does not vanish when search costs fall; what erodes is (a) the paid-ranking/"Tipp"-style steering margin (agents penalise sponsored labels) and (b) the marketing arbitrage (buying attention on Google/TV and reselling it as leads). What gains value is a trusted, machine-verifiable endorsement, because agents reward endorsements and concentrate demand.
- HUK's "Wechselautomat" complaint becomes sharper with agents: if an agent re-shops every renewal, per-switch acquisition commissions rise as a share of premium. Expect providers to push for lower, retention-linked or trail ("Bestand") commissions, or to price agent-acquired customers separately. This is speculation consistent with HUK/Allianz behaviour.
- With parity clauses gone in energy (Feb 2026), providers can now legally undercut Check24 in their own or an agent-direct channel. An agent that can query provider-direct endpoints will find those prices; an agent-native Check24 must therefore either get the best price by merit (exclusive/negotiated agent tariffs) or monetise something other than price discovery.
- GIPP shows the regulatory alternative to agents: banning price walking shrinks the loyalty penalty (and thus the savings an auto-switcher can capture). Germany has no GIPP equivalent, so the inertia rent remains available to buy-side agents; that is an inference from absence of a comparable rule in the sources found, to be checked by the regulation researcher.
- [background, not verified this session] Classic literature to cite after verification: Ellison & Ellison (2009, Econometrica) on obfuscation in price-search-engine markets; Gal & Elkin-Koren (2017, Harvard JOLT) "Algorithmic Consumers"; Edelman & Wright (2015) on price coherence/intermediary over-investment; Johnson (2017) and Wang & Wright on platform MFNs; CMA private motor insurance order (2015) banning wide MFNs; CMA v ComparetheMarket (GBP 17.9m fine in 2020, overturned by CAT 2022); CMA 2018 loyalty-penalty super-complaint response (~GBP 4bn/year across five markets).

- Full text of the Coasean Singularity paper (specific arguments on platform-owned vs. bring-your-own agents, blocking, monetisation) could not be read; nber.org must be allowed in the session's network settings.

## 2. Fiduciary (buy-side, user-paid) vs. commission (sell-side) models

### Takeaway
All three models exist in German energy switching today and Stiftung Warentest's rankings do not map cleanly to payment model: Wechselpilot charges the user 20% of realised savings, SwitchUp and remind.me are free and commission-funded, and the worst-reviewed behaviour (re-switching after five months) came from a commission-funded service. In the US, success fees of 35-60% of first-year savings (Rocket Money) show users will pay high contingent fees but generate complaints about inflated savings. A formal "duty of loyalty" for consumer agents is a live proposal (Stanford/Consumer Reports), not law.

### Cited Findings
- Wechselpilot: fee is 20% of the actual saving measured after one year (co-founder Maximilian Both); one consumer guide adds a EUR 200 cap and a 10% up-front payment (single source); it markets itself as 100% independent. A Finanztip forum user alleges it also earns commissions via portals such as Verivox (unverified user claim). Basicthinking (17 Nov 2025) headline: "up to EUR 450 per year" savings. — [biallo](https://www.biallo.de/energie/news/wechselhelfer/); [Finanztip forum](https://www.finanztip.de/community/forum/thema/4418-wechselpilot/); [basicthinking 17 Nov 2025](https://www.basicthinking.de/blog/2025/11/17/jaehrlich-bis-zu-450-euro-sparen-wechsel-mit-wechselpilot/)
- SwitchUp: free to the customer, takes commission from the new supplier, and states "we also recommend tariffs for which we get no commission". remind.me: free, financed solely by supplier commissions; Stiftung Warentest advises against remind.me, and a test report describes a contract it wanted to replace after only about five months. Industry norm for user-paid services is 20-30% of savings. — [test.de Wechselservices](https://www.test.de/gas-und-strom-wechselservice-test-5447465-0/); [t-online](https://www.t-online.de/finanzen/aktuelles/id_100874146/stiftung-warentest-einige-wechselservices-zeigen-schwaechen.html); [t-online](https://www.t-online.de/heim-garten/energie/id_87099976/was-koennen-wechseldienste-.html)
- Rocket Money (US): bill negotiation fee is 35-60% of first-year savings, user-chosen within the range, charged only on success, non-refundable. Third-party reviews report overcharging based on savings that did not materialise. — [Rocket Money help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/9744474-bill-negotiation-charge); [Motley Fool review](https://preview.www.fool.com/money/personal-finance/rocket-money-review)
- MONY Group (UK) hybrid/membership precedent, FY2025 (results 23 Feb 2026): SuperSaveClub passed 2.1m members, generates ~16% of group revenue, member ARPU GBP 35 vs GBP 20 for the wider base. — [RNS via LSE](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/MONY/preliminary-results-for-year-ended-31-dec-2025-jsnb4yxgt860i2b.html); [UK Investor Magazine](https://ukinvestormagazine.co.uk/?p=86534)
- "Loyal Agents" (Stanford Digital Economy Lab + Consumer Reports Innovation Lab, 2025-2026): distinguishes duty of care (avoid foreseeable harm) from duty of loyalty (fiduciary, put the principal first); a Stanford HAI issue brief argues developers/deployers of consumer agents, especially in financial services and healthcare, should be subject to a duty of loyalty; Consumer Reports' "Consumer Finance AI Standard" says the product's primary objective must be the consumer's financial interest and proposes an additional "duty of vigor". Consumer Reports itself notes loyalty is not yet defined; none of this is binding. — [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/loyal-agents); [Stanford HAI brief](https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/designing-loyalty-ai-agents-and-conflicts-of-interest); [CR Innovation](https://innovation.consumerreports.org/?p=11519)
- German licensing friction for LLM front ends: the intermediary association Votum has targeted ChatGPT and Claude for giving individual insurance recommendations without the 34d GewO licence. — [Cash-Online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/unerlaubte-ki-empfehlungen-votum-nimmt-chatgpt-und-claude-ins-visier-718600/)

### Inferences
- Payment model alone does not guarantee loyalty: churn-maximising behaviour (remind.me) is the failure mode of per-switch commissions; inflated-savings claims (Rocket Money) are the failure mode of share-of-savings. A design that is robust to both: net-of-commission disclosure, commission pass-through or rebating, savings measured ex post against the counterfactual renewal price (as Wechselpilot does after 12 months), and a cap.
- Share-of-savings has a structural weakness: revenue disappears when price dispersion collapses (see UK auto-switchers, section 5). A flat membership (MONY SuperSaveClub ARPU uplift of GBP 15) is more stable but small per head.
- A licensed intermediary (34d/34c/34f GewO, energy broker status) can carry the regulated "recommendation" that a general LLM front end may not be allowed to give in Germany; this makes the licence itself a distribution asset in an agent world (inference from the Votum complaint; legal outcome unknown).
- [background, not verified this session] Honorarberatung in Germany has stayed a niche (a few hundred registered Honorar-Finanzanlagenberater under 34h GewO versus tens of thousands commission-based intermediaries); German law lets brokers pass commissions through only within the limits of the Provisionsabgabeverbot (48b VAG). Look After My Bills was commission-funded and sold to GoCompare in 2019. The 2024 NAR settlement in the US decoupled buyer-agent commissions. Verify before use.

### Gaps
- No verified data on Honorarberatung market share, on German consumers' willingness to pay for household-contract management, or on Wechselpilot/SwitchUp customer numbers and revenue.
- No source found on Trim (Rocket Money was found; Trim not searched separately) or Permission Slip economics.

## 3. Agent-facing supply: catalogues, bindable quotes, reverse auctions and agent-to-agent negotiation

### Takeaway
Reverse-auction/collective-switching precedents (iChoosr) deliver real but modest and unreliable savings (UK average GBP 131 per household; some auctions produced no saving), and current LLM agents are poor shoppers in open marketplaces (first-offer bias, weaker agents lose in negotiation), so an agent-native intermediary should present agents with a small set of pre-verified, bindable, comparably structured offers rather than an open bazaar.

### Cited Findings
- Collective switching/reverse auctions: iChoosr runs reverse auctions with pre-vetted suppliers three times a year for UK councils; a UK government review of 27 schemes found total savings of GBP 2.7m, on average GBP 131 per household; individual schemes reported GBP 221 (North Tyneside 2014, ~10,000 households) and >GBP 350 (East Northants); in at least one Cambridgeshire round iChoosr could not secure any tariff that saved participants money. Figures are council/press self-reports. — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/collective-switching-saves-131-off-bills); [Warwickshire CC](https://www.warwickshire.gov.uk/energy/switch-save/2); [Cambridgeshire CC](https://www.cambridgeshire.gov.uk/residents/climate-change-energy-and-environment/how-you-can-take-action/home-energy/cambridgeshire-energy-switch)
- Agent-to-agent negotiation (simulation): "The Automated but Risky Game" (arXiv 2506.00073, June 2025) finds large disparities between agents; in Stanford HAI's summary buyers with weaker agents paid ~2% more and weaker seller agents lost up to 14% of profit versus equal-capability matchups; agents also got stuck in loops or quit early. — [arXiv 2506.00073](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00073v3); [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/the-art-of-the-automated-negotiation); [MIT Technology Review, 17 Jun 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/06/17/1118910/ai-price-negotiation/amp/)
- "Scaling Laws for Strategic Interactions" (ICML 2026; >5,000 games, 30+ models): higher capability raises joint efficiency and the surplus extracted from weaker agents; choosing a more capable agent is not guaranteed to be a Pareto improvement. PieArena (arXiv 2602.05302) finds scaffolding gives weaker models large catch-up gains. — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/78781); [arXiv 2602.05302](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05302v1)
- Magentic Marketplace: speed of response beat offer quality by 10-30x; more options reduced welfare. — [arXiv 2510.25779](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25779v1)
- Real-world agent-readable quoting in insurance: Insurify launched what it calls the first insurance app in ChatGPT's app directory in Feb 2026 (compare/estimate car insurance); an upgrade reported for Aug 2026 serves real-time personalised quotes in-chat from a network of 120+ insurance partners, rolling out to 40+ US states by end-2026 (date from a single source). The app stores no user data unless the user proceeds to finalise on Insurify's site. Tuio (Spain) offers a home-insurance ChatGPT app. — [Insurance Journal, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/02/10/857535.htm); [Insurify press](https://insurify.com/press/news/insurify-expands-chatgpt-plugin/); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4948281/ChatGPT-wird-zum-Versicherungsvermittler/)
- Germany status: per Finanztip, insurance can be concluded inside ChatGPT in the USA and Spain but not (yet) in Germany, for legal and data-protection reasons (article date not confirmed). — [Finanztip](https://www.finanztip.de/daily/versicherungen-per-chatgpt-veraendert-ki-jetzt-den-vertragsabschluss/)
- Comparison portals entering LLM front ends: MoneySuperMarket launched a ChatGPT app in 2025 under an OpenAI enterprise agreement ("a new route to market"; company claims first UK comparison site in the ChatGPT app store); Comparis (CH) launched a ChatGPT plug-in for flat search (22 Sep 2026). No Check24 ChatGPT app was found in search. — [MONY RNS](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/MONY/preliminary-results-for-year-ended-31-dec-2025-jsnb4yxgt860i2b.html); [Netzwoche, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.netzwoche.ch/news/2026-09-22/comparis-lanciert-chatgpt-plug-in-fuer-die-wohnungssuche)
- The Coasean Singularity paper argues agents lower the cost of preference elicitation, contract enforcement and identity verification, expanding feasible market designs. — [NBER WP 34468](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34468)

### Inferences
- Design implication of first-offer bias and "more options, lower welfare": the intermediary's value to a consumer's general-purpose agent is curation plus a guarantee, i.e. return 3-5 normalised, bindable offers with a signed quote ID, validity window, total-cost-of-ownership figure (bonus-adjusted, year-2 price), eligibility already checked, and machine-readable key terms (Laufzeit, Kündigungsfrist, Preisgarantie scope, Selbstbeteiligung, exclusions).
- A reverse auction on verified intent ("household X, 3,500 kWh, switch date 1 March, SCHUFA-OK, pays by SEPA") is technically cheap for agents, but iChoosr's failed rounds show sellers only bid when margin exists; expect auctions to work in motor (annual 30 Nov cycle) and energy in falling wholesale markets, and to fail in tight markets.
- Because weaker agents lose in negotiation, a consumer whose front end is a free-tier LLM benefits from delegating the negotiation leg to a specialised intermediary agent with scale and data; this is a defensible role for an agent-native Check24 (speculative; based on simulation studies only).
- [background, not verified this session] Precedents to verify: Priceline "name your own price" (retired for flights 2016), Doc Searls' VRM/"Intention Economy" (2012) intentcasting, Honey (acquired by PayPal for ~USD 4bn, 2020), Which? "Big Switch" 2012, Verbraucherzentrale-backed Sammelwechsel schemes in Germany.

## 4. Data moats and defensible assets when ChatGPT/Gemini are the front end

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on which assets stay defensible is thin; the strongest signals are that LLMs alone are still weak at rate comparison in Germany (Biallo test), that front ends cannot yet bind insurance in Germany and face a licensing challenge, and that Check24 holds a 60-70% share of portal energy conclusions, which implies proprietary conversion, switch-outcome and provider-integration data that no LLM vendor has.

### Cited Findings
- General LLMs are weak at live German rate comparison: a Biallo test of AI interest-rate comparison found answers often missed the best savings accounts, with ChatGPT particularly weak (reported via Echo24; date not confirmed). — [Biallo](https://www.biallo.de/geldanlage/news/ki-zinsvergleich/); [Echo24](https://www.echo24.de/welt/ki-beim-zinsvergleich-staerken-und-schwaechen-im-test-zr-94206535.html)
- Regulatory perimeter as moat: insurance cannot be concluded in ChatGPT in Germany (Finanztip); Votum complaint that chatbots give individual insurance recommendations without a 34d GewO licence. — [Finanztip](https://www.finanztip.de/daily/versicherungen-per-chatgpt-veraendert-ki-jetzt-den-vertragsabschluss/); [Cash-Online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/unerlaubte-ki-empfehlungen-votum-nimmt-chatgpt-und-claude-ins-visier-718600/)
- Provider integration share: 60-70% of energy conclusions via portals attributed to Check24 (Vertriebskanalstudie 2025 via ZfK). — [ZfK](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/vertrieb/macht-der-portale-check24-verivox-preisvergleich-energie)
- Supply gaps weaken the moat: HUK-Coburg/HUK24 tariffs are absent from Check24 because HUK refuses to cooperate; Check24 lost litigation over how it displayed HUK tariffs (OLG Köln) and, per a later report, must specially safeguard cancellations of HUK policies made via the portal (LG Berlin). — [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4879665/Check24-unterliegt-HUK-vor-dem-OLG-Koln/); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4889645/Check24-verliert-Rechtsstreit-gegen-Huk/)
- Providers already keep their own switching-history data to screen customers: Vattenfall stores customer data for five years to identify and reject bonus hunters (Stiftung Warentest). — [test.de](https://www.test.de/Strom-und-Gastarife-Vattenfall-siebt-Kunden-aus-5859473-0/)
- First-party data via membership/giveaways: MONY's SuperSaveClub members (2.1m) yield higher ARPU (GBP 35 vs 20); Check24 sent five million free jerseys to customers for Euro 2024 and repeated the mechanic for the 2026 World Cup, described as a data and reach play. — [UK Investor Magazine](https://ukinvestormagazine.co.uk/?p=86534); [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/cases/check24-trikot-wm-trikot-daten-marketing-coup)
- Agent choice is unstable and manipulable (model updates reshuffle shares; seller-side description tweaks win share), so verified, hard-to-fake signals have value. — [arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630v3)

### Inferences
- Likely durable (researcher's assessment, speculative): (1) licences plus liability-bearing advice and documentation; (2) bind/execute rails into providers (application, SEPA mandate, Lieferantenwechsel process, eVB number, cancellation service) and the resulting switch-success and rejection data; (3) the logged-in contract inventory with renewal dates, which enables proactive, standing-mandate re-shopping that a stateless LLM chat cannot do; (4) proprietary service-quality data (claims handling, complaint and rejection rates, bonus payout reliability) that lets an agent optimise on more than price; (5) negotiated exclusive tariffs.
- Likely eroding: brand-driven direct traffic and TV-built top-of-mind (agents do not watch TV), SEO/SEA arbitrage, ranking-position sales, and UI-based steering.
- Consumption and risk data (smart meter, telematics, SCHUFA, claims history) are mostly held by third parties and portable under GDPR Art. 20 / EU Data Act / FiDA-type regimes, so they are a moat only if the intermediary is the consented aggregator the consumer's agent calls; the regulation researcher should confirm status.
- HUK's absence shows the catalogue is not complete; a consumer agent that can also query HUK24 directly will see a strictly larger market than Check24. Completeness (including non-commission tariffs, as SwitchUp claims to show) becomes a credibility requirement in an agent world.

### Gaps

- No evidence found on LLM vendors acquiring comparable provider integrations in Germany.

## 5. Architecture and company precedents (incl. failed UK auto-switchers)

### Takeaway
The clearest cautionary precedent is UK energy auto-switching: Flipper closed in September 2021 and Look After My Bills paused and then stopped auto-switching when wholesale price spikes and the price cap eliminated cheap tariffs, showing that a single-vertical, share-of-savings or per-switch model has no revenue when price dispersion collapses. The clearest positive precedents in 2026 are comparison/quote engines shipping as apps inside ChatGPT (Insurify, Tuio, MoneySuperMarket, Comparis).

### Cited Findings
- Flipper (UK, subscription auto-switcher) announced on 28 Sep 2021 that it was withdrawing from the market and closing, because wholesale gas price rises and the loss of cheap tariffs meant it could not sustain promised savings. — [Money To The Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/quick-savings/utilities/flipper-review-is-it-the-best-energy-auto-switching-site)
- Look After My Bills told reviewers in 2022 it had temporarily paused energy switching for lack of cheap deals; a secondary site reports it is no longer operating and existing users are no longer auto-switched, attributing the model's failure to price caps, supplier collapses and thin margins leaving savings often under GBP 30 and little commission. Secondary sources; no company or Ofgem confirmation found. — [Money To The Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/quick-savings/utilities/look-after-my-bills-review-is-it-the-best-energy-switching-service/amp); [householdmoneysaving.com](https://www.householdmoneysaving.com/look-after-my-bills-review/)
- Switchcraft status is conflicting: its Trustpilot page describes automatic switching as ongoing and includes a complaint alleging unauthorised switches. — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/switchcraft.co.uk)
- German Wechselservice landscape tested by Stiftung Warentest (multiple rounds; two services ranked ahead in the latest test; Wechselpilot was "very recommendable" in older tests but did not meet all criteria later; remind.me advised against). — [test.de](https://www.test.de/gas-und-strom-wechselservice-test-5447465-0/)
- Insurify ChatGPT app (Feb 2026; real-time quotes Aug 2026, 120+ carriers); broker share prices fell on the launch and Insurify's CEO called the reaction an overreaction (S&P Global). Börsen-Zeitung: start of insurance sales via ChatGPT hit share prices across the industry; German insurers continue to rely on direct customer contact. — [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/02/10/857535.htm); [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/2/insurify-ceo-flags-overreaction-to-new-ai-app-as-brokers-take-a-hit-98554977); [Börsen-Zeitung](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/banken-finanzen/ki-soll-versicherern-den-kundenkontakt-erleichtern)
- MoneySuperMarket ChatGPT app and OpenAI enterprise agreement (2025); Comparis ChatGPT plug-in (Sep 2026). — [MONY RNS](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/MONY/preliminary-results-for-year-ended-31-dec-2025-jsnb4yxgt860i2b.html); [Netzwoche](https://www.netzwoche.ch/news/2026-09-22/comparis-lanciert-chatgpt-plug-in-fuer-die-wohnungssuche)
- OpenAI "Sponsored Agents" (tested with select US advertisers, 2026): users click an ad and start a labelled conversation with a business's own agent inside ChatGPT. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/reimagining-advertising-with-ai/)
- Check24 strategic pruning: per Hypoport/Börsen-Zeitung, Check24 is withdrawing from mortgage brokerage to concentrate on scalable models. — [Börsen-Zeitung](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/banken-finanzen/hypoport-bekraeftigt-jahresziele-nach-schwaecherem-zweiten-quartal)

### Inferences
- Lessons from UK auto-switchers for a German design: (1) diversify across verticals so one market's dispersion collapse (energy crisis) does not zero revenue; (2) have a revenue line not tied to switching (membership, servicing, claims/cancellation help); (3) auto-switching exposes the intermediary to counterparty risk (supplier failures) and to complaints about unauthorised switches, so mandate evidence and supplier solvency screening are core features; (4) regulation that compresses dispersion (price caps, GIPP-style rules) is an existential risk to pure savings-share models.
- "App inside the LLM" is today's architecture: the intermediary keeps the quote engine, licence and bind flow; the LLM owns the conversation. Insurify's choice to push finalisation to its own site shows intermediaries trying to retain the customer record.
- Sponsored Agents suggest the LLM platforms will sell conversational entry points; an agent-native Check24 would be a buyer of that inventory, recreating the Google SEA dependency in a new form (inference).

### Gaps
- Searches for 2025-2026 European startups building "AI agent for household contracts/bills" returned only B2B finance-agent rounds (e.g. Agent F, Causa Prima); I found no reliable source on a funded consumer household-contract agent in Europe. This is absence of search evidence, not evidence of absence.

## 6. Trust and UX for delegated purchasing; willingness to delegate (Germany)

### Takeaway
German consumers use AI heavily for research but overwhelmingly refuse full delegation today: about 6% would fully delegate a purchase (Deloitte), 16% accept an agent completing a purchase directly after a chat instruction, and 93% want to be able to stop an AI's purchase decision at any time (Riverty/Adyen); in insurance, 54% prefer personal service and 50% say insurers using AI are less trustworthy (YouGov). Design must therefore default to "approve" with a kill switch, with auto-execute as an earned opt-in.

### Cited Findings
- Deloitte (European survey, ~13,500 respondents): 52% of German users used AI shopping aids in May 2026 (44% in Sep 2025); Germany is mid-table (Spain 67%, Poland 63%, Italy 60%); just under 6% of German users would fully delegate the purchase; trust is the main barrier. — [Deloitte DE](https://www.deloitte.com/de/de/about/press-room/jeder-zweite-in-europa-shoppt-mit-KI-untersuetzung.html); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/dk/en/Industries/consumer/perspectives/ai-x-retail-the-state-of-agentic-commerce-in-europe.html)

### Inferences
- A defensible autonomy ladder for Germany: L0 monitor and alert (contract inventory, renewal deadlines); L1 recommend with explanation and net-of-commission disclosure; L2 prepare and one-tap approve (default); L3 auto-execute inside a standing mandate (per-vertical price ceiling, minimum saving threshold, provider white/blacklist, minimum quality score, no longer lock-in than current) with pre-notification and a stop button; L4 full autonomy only for low-risk, reversible products.
- German statutory cooling-off rights (14-day Widerruf for distance contracts; longer for life insurance) are a natural "undo" layer the UX can surface; the regulation researcher should confirm details and the 2026 Widerrufsbutton rules.
- Given 93% demand for a stop function and 6% full-delegation willingness, near-term revenue should be modelled on approve-mode conversion, not on autonomous switching volume; treat auto-execute adoption as an upside scenario (speculative).
- The delegation gap by product (groceries higher, luxury 10%) plus insurance trust data suggests sequencing: energy and telco (commodity, reversible) first, motor next, then advice-heavy lines (BU, PKV, Baufi) remaining human-in-the-loop.

## 8. Risks

### Takeaway
Documented risks are provider retaliation against frequent switchers (Vattenfall screens out bonus hunters; HUK and Allianz withdrew from or stopped paying Check24), model-collapse when price dispersion disappears (UK auto-switchers), manipulable and biased agents (first-offer bias, prompt injection, description gaming), unequal agent capability, and re-intermediation by LLM gatekeepers who now sell ads and sponsored agents. Algorithmic-collusion risk is well established in the literature but was not verified in this session.

### Cited Findings
- Provider retaliation: Vattenfall stores customer data for five years to identify and reject bonus hunters; some suppliers reject customers outright (Stiftung Warentest). Courts have struck down opaque bonus-exclusion clauses (LG Köln v 365 AG/almado), and the BGH (July 2023, VIII ZR 267/20) confirmed bonus entitlement for customers of insolvent BEV; no ruling was found that prohibits or permits rejecting switchers as such. — [test.de](https://www.test.de/Strom-und-Gastarife-Vattenfall-siebt-Kunden-aus-5859473-0/); [Verbraucherzentrale NRW](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.nrw/aktuelle-meldungen/energie/neukundenbonus-der-almadoenergy-landgericht-kippt-klauseln-12919); [vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/urteile/bgh-kundinnen-des-insolventen-versorgers-bev-steht-neukundenbonus-zu)
- Supply withdrawal: HUK-Coburg left portals (2017) and says it does not want to "feed the switching machine"; HUK-Coburg's CEO argued comparison portals drive up motor insurance prices; Allianz cut Check24 commission. — [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4879665/chapter/1/Check24-unterliegt-HUK-vor-dem-OLG-Koln/); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4863571/HUK-Coburg-Heitmann-Vergleichsportale-Autoversicherung/)
- Model collapse in a crisis: Flipper closed 28 Sep 2021; Look After My Bills paused in 2022 and later stopped auto-switching. — [Money To The Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/quick-savings/utilities/flipper-review-is-it-the-best-energy-auto-switching-site); [householdmoneysaving.com](https://www.householdmoneysaving.com/look-after-my-bills-review/)
- Over-switching and conduct risk: remind.me proposing a new contract after about five months (Stiftung Warentest advises against); Switchcraft user complaint of unauthorised switches; Rocket Money complaints of fees on unrealised savings and "messed up" accounts. — [test.de](https://www.test.de/gas-und-strom-wechselservice-test-5447465-0/); [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/switchcraft.co.uk); [Motley Fool](https://preview.www.fool.com/money/personal-finance/rocket-money-review)
- Agent manipulation and bias: susceptibility to fake credentials, fake reviews and prompt injection varies by model (Magentic Marketplace; which models resisted differs between secondary reports); seller-side description tweaks shift share and model updates reshuffle shares (ACES). German trade press already runs "how insurers increase their visibility in ChatGPT & Co." and "which insurers AI recommends most often" pieces, i.e. AEO/GEO optimisation is under way. — [arXiv 2510.25779](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25779v1); [arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630v3); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4951238/So-erhoehen-Versicherer-ihre-Sichtbarkeit-bei-ChatGPT--Co/); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4951009/Diese-Versicherer-empfiehlt-die-KI-besonders-haeufig/)
- Capability inequality: weaker agents pay more or lose profit against stronger ones; "users using less capable agents [face] significant financial loss". — [arXiv 2506.00073](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00073v3); [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/78781)
- Gatekeeper dependence: OpenAI monetises via ads (nine markets by Aug 2026), sponsored agents and a 4% checkout fee; search competition already compresses MONY's marketing margin. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/reimagining-advertising-with-ai/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/ecommerce/2026/shopify-merchants-to-pay-4percent-fee-on-sales-made-through-chatgpt-checkout); [Josh Thompson](https://joshthompson.co.uk/investing/mony-group-fy25-results-ai-integration-new-buyback/)
- Licensing/liability exposure of unlicensed AI advice in Germany (Votum v ChatGPT/Claude over 34d GewO). — [Cash-Online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/unerlaubte-ki-empfehlungen-votum-nimmt-chatgpt-und-claude-ins-visier-718600/)
- Quality side effects of price-focused regulation/competition: after GIPP, motor compulsory excesses rose (FCA EP25/2), a marker of quality dilution when headline price is the comparison axis. — [FCA EP25/2](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/corporate-documents/evaluation-paper-25-2-general-insurance-pricing-practices-remedies)
- Congestion and obfuscation are named as agent-induced frictions by Shahidi et al. — [NBER WP 34468](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34468)

### Inferences
- Adverse selection: agent-managed customers are by construction the most price-elastic; providers will respond with (a) rejection or exclusion from new-customer bonuses (already practised), (b) channel-specific tariffs without bonus but with lower run-rate price, (c) lower or deferred commissions with clawback. A sustainable design aligns with providers on customer lifetime: optimise multi-year total cost, prefer no-bonus fair tariffs, offer providers a "retention bid" before switching away, and pay or receive trail-based rather than per-switch remuneration (speculative design guidance).
- Race to the bottom on quality: if agents rank on price, providers hollow out cover (cf. rising excesses post-GIPP; CMA "hollowing out" concern, unverified here). Countermeasure: the agent must score on standardised quality and service data, which is exactly the proprietary dataset an intermediary can own.
- First-offer bias means sellers will compete on latency and on being first in the agent's context; a curated intermediary endpoint that returns a ranked, bounded set removes that attack surface for the consumer agent.
- Liability for bad switches (coverage gaps, lost bonuses, double contracts, failed Lieferantenwechsel) sits naturally with the licensed intermediary, not the LLM front end; carrying it (guarantees such as "we pay the difference/any double payment") is a differentiator but needs reserves. No source found quantifying such liabilities.
- [background, not verified this session] Algorithmic collusion literature to verify and cite: Calvano, Calzolari, Denicolò, Pastorello (AER 2020) on Q-learning collusion; Assad, Clark, Ershov, Xu (JPE 2024) on German retail gasoline margins after algorithmic-pricing adoption; Fish, Gonczarowski, Shorrer on LLM pricing agents reaching supra-competitive prices; Monopolkommission 2018 Hauptgutachten chapter on algorithms and collusion.

- No documented prompt-injection incident against a comparison or switching agent found.
