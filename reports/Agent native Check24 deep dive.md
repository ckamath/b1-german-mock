# An agent-native Check24 trades clicks for mandates

An agent-native Check24 is not a comparison website with a chatbot. It is a licensed household contract agent: it holds a standing, revocable Vollmacht over a customer's energy, insurance, telco and banking contracts, exposes bindable quotes and switch execution to any AI front end through an MCP/API layer, and re-optimises contracts continuously inside limits the customer sets. The case for this design rests on three findings. First, as of November 2026 the buyer-side agent is real for research but not for purchase: **52% of Germans use AI shopping aids, yet just under 6% would fully delegate a purchase**, and no major platform's agentic checkout is live in Germany. Second, every shipped agent protocol (ACP, UCP, AP2, the card-network programmes) models a one-off retail checkout of a priced SKU; none models a quote from personal data, a regulated advice step, a SEPA mandate, a cancellation or a supplier switch, which is exactly the work a contract intermediary does. Third, German law (broker licensing, text-form powers of attorney, advice-documentation duties) keeps the liability-bearing step with a licensed intermediary and currently keeps general-purpose assistants out of it. What erodes is the top of Check24's funnel, the bought attention on Google and TV; what survives is the licensed execution layer and the logged-in contract inventory. The recommended business model is a hybrid: commission stays as the base, but is disclosed, made ranking-neutral and credited to the customer where the law allows, with a membership fee as the revenue line that does not depend on switching. Readers should weigh all of this against the evidence base: nearly every fact below was verified only at search-extract level, Check24's financials are largely undisclosed, and the design, economics and roadmap sections are proposals and estimates, not findings.

## How to read this report: three confidence levels

The researchers could run web searches but could not open most pages; fetches of primary sources (protocol specifications, law-firm briefings, Wikipedia, Stiftung Warentest, NBER, McKinsey and others) were blocked. The only document read in full was Check24's own GitHub recruiting challenge. Statute and judgment references link to official texts but were cited from the researchers' knowledge and not re-read. Version numbers, dates and article numbers should be re-checked against primary sources before anyone relies on them.

This report therefore uses three labels throughout. **Sourced** means a claim carries a link and was seen in a search extract of that source. **Single-source or unverified** means one secondary source, a conflict between sources, or a figure the researchers flagged as doubtful; these are marked in the text. **Proposal or estimate** means the claim is this report's design judgment or the researchers' own arithmetic; sections 4, 5 and 9 consist almost entirely of such material and are marked as such. Claims the researchers held only from memory without a source have been left out.

## Check24 today: bought attention resold as undisclosed commission

### The scale is real but the numbers are a decade old

No confirmed current revenue, profit or valuation for Check24 exists in the sources found. The last hard figures are **EUR 652m revenue for 2016/17, up from EUR 497m the year before**; a EUR 15bn valuation that circulates is one journalist's extrapolation of roughly 20% annual growth, not a transaction ([deutsche-startups.de](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2023/09/06/startupticker-check24-speekly-fintech/)). Low single-digit billions of revenue today is a plausible extrapolation and nothing more. Third-party estimates such as Similarweb's USD 75-100m are plainly wrong against the 2016/17 figure and should not be used ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/company/check24.de)). Employee counts conflict, from "more than 1,000 (2021)" to an estimated 5,289 in September 2025 ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check24); [Revelio Labs](https://www.reveliolabs.com/companies/check24-comparison/employees)).

The shape of the business is better established. Check24 is 90% management-owned per a Wikipedia-derived extract ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check24)) and is organised as a network of **around 90 GmbHs that run their own profit-and-loss accounts almost independently** ([Abendzeitung München](https://www.abendzeitung-muenchen.de/muenchen/gehaelter-bei-check24-top-verdienst-oder-durchschnitt-art-1123735)). Check24 describes itself as "many independent comparison products ('verticals') that customers experience as one brand" ([Check24 GitHub](https://github.com/check24-scholarships/check24-chatgpt-app-challenge)). The verticals span insurance, finance, energy, telecoms, travel, shopping and handyman services ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check24)), plus C24 Bank with its own banking licence ([Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4893026/Check24-mit-Banklizenz/)). C24 is also a liability: in November 2025 BaFin appointed a special commissioner there for "massive deficiencies in money-laundering prevention" ([Finanztip](https://www.finanztip.de/erfahrungen/c24-bank/)). No revenue split by vertical was found.

### The model: provider-paid, success-based, undisclosed

Check24 earns a commission from the provider when a contract is concluded; in insurance it sits inside the premium and the customer pays nothing separately ([Check24](https://www.check24.de/unternehmen/ueber-uns/?product=2)). It declines to disclose commission levels ([Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/tag/Vergleichsportal/step/3/)). The only hard numbers are downstream: Check24 paid affiliates **EUR 70 per brokered motor policy** in the 2025 switching season and advertises "up to EUR 125 and more" per sale across products ([Check24 Partnerprogramm](https://www.check24-partnerprogramm.de/affiliate-marketing/ankuendigungen/kfz-wechselsaison-2025-mit-70-euro-provision-pro-sale/); [Check24 Partnerprogramm](https://www.check24.de/partner/partnerprogramm/)). Those payouts set a logical floor under what insurers pay Check24. An old press figure puts insurer payments at EUR 70-100 per concluded contract, with product scope unclear ([Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4858949/Huk-Coburg-Vergleichsportal-Ausstieg-teuer/)). Actual commission levels per vertical remain unknown.

The cost side is bought attention. Gross ad spend was EUR 171m in 2018 per Nielsen, and a secondary source reports over EUR 150m on TV alone in 2022 ([Reise vor9](https://www.reisevor9.de/marketing-digitales/wie-check24-mit-marketing-millionen-den-markt-erobert)). An old estimate, which its own author calls uncertain, put Google spend at EUR 100m or more against roughly EUR 330m revenue around 2015/16 ([Netz-Trends](https://www.netz-trends.de/id/5509/Umsatz-von-330-Miobei-Check24-Dank-100-Mio-Euro-Marketingausgaben/)). On those numbers, paid acquisition consumed about a third of revenue. That is the structural cost an agent channel either removes or redirects to a new gatekeeper.

### Seasonality, channel power and a standing supply gap

Motor insurance is the origin product and the most seasonal: the switching season ends 30 November, and about **1.7 million motor contracts changed provider in the 2025 season**, with portals ("mostly Check24 or Verivox") winning a bit over a quarter of new business, per a summary whose wording the researchers found ambiguous ([Das Investment](https://www.dasinvestment.com/kfz-versicherung-wechselsaison-vertragswechsler-sirius-campus/)). In energy, a distribution-channel study cited by trade press attributes **60-70% of portal contract conclusions to Check24** ([ZfK](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/vertrieb/macht-der-portale-check24-verivox-preisvergleich-energie)). That power drew the Bundeskartellamt: proceedings over price-parity clauses opened in July 2025, and on 24 February 2026 Check24's commitments became binding, so neither the contract nor the commission level can depend on a supplier's prices in other channels ([LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/kanzleien-unternehmen/k/check24-gibt-preisparitaetsklauseln-nach-ermittlungen-des-bkarta-auf); [Rödl](https://www.roedl.com/insights/check24-bestpreisklauseln-bundeskartellamt/)). Suppliers can now legally undercut Check24 in their own channel.

The catalogue is incomplete. HUK-Coburg, Germany's largest motor insurer, left all comparison portals from October 2017, objecting that portals earn a fresh acquisition commission every time a customer re-shops ([Autohaus](https://www.autohaus.de/nachrichten/kfz-versicherer-huk-coburg-kehrt-vergleichsportalen-den-ruecken-2018446.html); [Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4879665/chapter/1/Check24-unterliegt-HUK-vor-dem-OLG-Koln/)). A Cologne court found Check24 could offer the cheapest tariff in only about 80% of cases when it prohibited the "Nirgendwo-Günstiger-Garantie" ([Horizont](https://www.horizont.net/marketing/nachrichten/werbeversprechen-gericht-verbietetcheck24die-nirgendwo-guenstiger-garantie-182526)). The Bundeskartellamt's 2019 sector inquiry found rankings partly driven by commission, non-paying providers mostly not shown, and conduct that "does not match the ideal of a neutral platform" ([Bundeskartellamt](https://bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2019/11_04_2019_Vergleichsportale.html?nn=48916)).

### Check24 is already moving toward agents, quietly

Check24 runs an AI assistant, "Sophie", across verticals; a vendor blog (not an official source) says it handles logged-in customers' own cases and hands over to human advisers ([LoyJoy](https://www.loyjoy.com/en/blog/check24-sophie-ai-customer-service-agent/)). A Check24 job ad names the goal of AI agents that "increasingly resolve matters independently", with degree of automation as a KPI ([Check24 Karriere](https://jobs.check24.de/en/jobs/produkt-projektmanagement/REF615Y-junior-produktmanager-mwd-ai-chatbot-mietwagen/)). The company founded "Check24 AI Forge GmbH" ([cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/special-digitalisierung-kuenstliche-intelligenz-oder-doch-natuerliche-700325/3/)).

The most revealing document is a 2026 recruiting challenge, the one source read in full. It asks applicants to build a proxy MCP that gives ChatGPT a single connector in front of several independent vertical MCP servers, because "each vertical owns its own MCP server" and defines its own tools and schemas, while customer requests "often start from a life situation or goal rather than a chosen product" ([Check24 GitHub](https://github.com/check24-scholarships/check24-chatgpt-app-challenge)). A recruiting challenge is not a launch; a live Check24 ChatGPT app is probable (a third-party listing describes one for travel) but unconfirmed ([Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/ai/best-chatgpt-travel-apps/)). The document does show Check24's central obstacle in its own words: agent conversations cross verticals, while tools, schemas and P&Ls are owned vertical by vertical. No evidence was found that Check24 itself holds standing mandates and switches contracts without the customer initiating each time.

## Agentic commerce in November 2026: research is real, purchase is not

### Platforms retreated from checkout and moved to discovery plus ads

No platform has made autonomous in-chat checkout work at scale. OpenAI launched Instant Checkout in the US on 29 September 2025 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt/)) and in March 2026 moved away from it: purchases now complete on merchant-owned sites and apps. Walmart reported conversion **three times lower for items sold inside ChatGPT** than for those that sent users to its own site ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/03/24/openai-agentic-commerce-updates-chatgpt-walmart/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)). Whether Instant Checkout is fully gone or still available to some approved partners is contested between sources ([OpenAI developers](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/production)). Google's UCP-powered checkout covers products eligible in the US, Canada and Australia only ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/16837055?hl=en)). Meta's "Muse" agent launched US-only in September 2026 ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse)). Amazon blocks dozens of external agents ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/21/amazon-meta-muse-ai-agentic-shopping)), and its case against Perplexity is unresolved: an injunction granted in March 2026 was vacated by the Ninth Circuit in August on the reasoning that the user, not Perplexity, accesses Amazon, a ruling limited to the current record ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-10/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexity-s-ai-shopping-bots); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/perplexity-ai-shopping-agent-returns-amazon/)).

For Germany, the only confirmed consumer-facing commercial change is advertising: ChatGPT Ads started on 24 August 2026 as part of a 31-market European rollout, per a third-party guide ([amzplus-consulting](https://amzplus-consulting.de/blog/marktplatz-chatgpt-ads-deutschland-start-2026)). No start date for in-chat purchase in Germany was found ([sichtbarerwerden.de](https://sichtbarerwerden.de/ratgeber/ki-ads/chatgpt-shopping/)). Stripe announced at its Berlin event that German businesses can sell inside AI interfaces "from later in 2026"; no source confirms it shipped ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/stripe-tour-berlin-2026)). OpenAI is also testing "Sponsored Agents", where a user clicks an ad and talks to a business's own agent inside ChatGPT ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/reimagining-advertising-with-ai/)). The control point has shifted from who owns checkout to who owns discovery and sells the slot, which is structurally the comparison portal's own lead model.

### Adoption: heavy research use, single-digit delegation

AI referral traffic grows fast from a tiny base and converts well. Adobe measured AI traffic to US retail sites up 693% year on year in the 2025 holiday season and 393% in Q1 2026, slowing to roughly 60-70% by mid-2026 ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/01/13/generative-ai-online-holiday-shopping-traffic-2025/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/16/ai-traffic-to-us-retailers-rose-393-in-q1-and-its-boosting-their-revenue-too/); [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/08/19/adobe-ai-referral-traffic-data-july-2026/)). Bain puts AI at up to a quarter of referral traffic for some retailers but under 1% of total traffic ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/agentic-ai-in-retail-how-autonomous-shopping-redefining-customer-journey/)). Booking Holdings said in its Q3 2026 results that AI referrals are significantly under 1% of room nights and not growing noticeably faster ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/finance/booking-holdings-q3-2026-earnings)). No source gave a hard number for agent-completed checkout volume anywhere. The large forecasts (McKinsey's USD 3-5tn globally by 2030, Bain's USD 300-500bn for the US) count purchases "orchestrated" or "influenced" by agents and are projections ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-agentic-commerce-opportunity-how-ai-agents-are-ushering-in-a-new-era-for-consumers-and-merchants); [Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/2030-forecast-how-agentic-ai-will-reshape-us-retail-snap-chart/)).

German consumers draw a sharp line between research and delegation. Deloitte found 52% using AI shopping aids in May 2026 (44% in September 2025) and just under 6% willing to fully delegate ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/de/de/about/press-room/jeder-zweite-in-europa-shoppt-mit-KI-untersuetzung.html)). A Riverty/Adyen survey found **93% want to be able to stop an AI's purchase decision at any time** ([Lebensmittelpraxis](https://lebensmittelpraxis.de/handel-aktuell/47604-kuenstliche-intelligenz-deutsche-fordern-kontrolle-ueber-ki-einkaeufe.html)). In insurance, 47% would most likely use comparison portals, only 2% of over-55s rely on AI chat ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/de-de/artikel/53288-ki-vergleichsportale-vertrauen-was-versicherer-jetzt-wissen-mussen)), and 54% prefer personal service ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/de-de/artikel/55740-ki-im-versicherungswesen-kratzt-am-image-fur-standardprozesse-akzeptiert-doch-mehrheit-will-personliche-betreuung)). The top of the funnel is shifting all the same: one trade-press survey reports 16% of AI users find a suitable motor policy directly via AI ([Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4948671/Kuenstliche-Intelligenz-veraendert-Tarifsuche-in-der-Kfz-Versicherung/)). No survey asked Germans specifically about letting an agent auto-switch insurance, energy or telco contracts; the figures above concern shopping in general.

### Protocols: a retail SKU stack with a contract-shaped hole

Four layers have settled. **MCP** is the tool-connection layer; its 2026-07-28 release made the core stateless, hardened OAuth/OIDC authorization and moved UI ("MCP Apps") and long-running "Tasks" into extensions, under Linux Foundation governance ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/); [AAIF](https://aaif.io/blog/authorization-in-mcp-2026-07-28-clarifying-trust-relationships-for-agentic-systems)). **A2A** reached v1.0 in March 2026 with signed agent cards ([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/a2a-protocol-surpasses-150-organizations-lands-in-major-cloud-platforms-and-sees-enterprise-production-use-in-first-year)). **ACP** (OpenAI and Stripe) is a beta retail-checkout spec, latest stable version 2026-04-17, built from a product feed, a merchant-hosted checkout session and a delegated payment token scoped to merchant, amount and expiry ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/protocols/acp); [OpenAI Delegated Payment Spec](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/payment)). **UCP** (Google with Shopify, January 2026) has merchants publish a capability profile at `/.well-known/ucp` and negotiate namespaced capabilities such as checkout, cart, catalog, order and OAuth identity linking ([UCP](https://ucp.dev/documentation/core-concepts/)). **AP2** is the authorization layer: user-signed credentials prove what an agent was allowed to buy. Its v0.2 defines Checkout and Payment Mandates in "closed" (final, human present) and "open" (constraints signed once, agent executes later) forms secured as SD-JWTs, and Google contributed it to the FIDO Alliance alongside Mastercard's Verifiable Intent ([AP2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md); [FIDO Alliance](https://fidoalliance.org/building-the-trust-layer-for-agentic-payments-with-ap2-and-verifiable-intent/)). The date of v0.2 (28 April 2026) rests on a single secondary source.

The researchers' gap map is the most important protocol finding, and it is an inference from scope statements, not proof of absence. Payment authorization is over-served: ACP tokens, AP2, Visa, Mastercard, x402 and KYAPay all address it. None of the specs was found to model a quote computed from rich personal data, regulated pre-contractual steps, contract conclusion with a later start date or underwriting decision, SEPA mandate capture, cancellation of an incumbent third-party contract, or switch orchestration. A targeted search for agent protocols covering insurance or other regulated products returned nothing. The missing primitive for a switching platform is a machine-verifiable authority to conclude and terminate contracts, a Vollmacht, not a payment token. AP2's open mandate and GLEIF's proposed Agent Mandate Credentials are the nearest reusable designs ([GLEIF](https://www.gleif.org/organizational-identity/research-publications/2026-08-13_agentic_ai_in_payments_v1.0-1.pdf)), and UCP's namespaced capability model, already being extended to food and lodging, is the most plausible place to standardise quote, bind, cancel and switch ([W3C workshop slides](https://www.w3.org/2026/09/ecommerce-agents/slides/Amit_Handa.pdf)).

### Payments: card pilots in a country that does not pay by card

European agent payments are card-network-led. Mastercard announced Germany's first live authenticated agentic transaction on 13 May 2026 with Deutsche Bank, DZ Bank and N26, and says all its European issuers are enabled at network level for Agent Pay; everything reported for Germany is pilot or controlled ([Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/news/europe/de-de/newsroom/pressemitteilungen/de-de/2026/deutschlands-erste-agentische-transaktion/); [Mastercard Europe](https://www.mastercard.com/news/europe/en/perspectives/en/2026/europe-is-building-the-foundations-for-trusted-agentic-commerce/)). Visa launched its "Agentic Ready" programme in Europe on 17 March 2026 on its Trusted Agent Protocol ([Visa](https://www.visa.co.uk/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.3437886.html)).

German online payments run elsewhere: **PayPal 28.7%, purchase on account 26.1%, direct debit 14.4%, cards 13.7%** of online sales per EHI ([DMEXCO](https://dmexco.com/stories/can-new-online-payment-provider-wero-challenge-paypal/)). Roughly 70% of volume therefore sits on methods with no documented agent-token scheme, and no source showed an agent-payment scheme on SEPA direct debit, Wero or invoice. For Check24's contract verticals this matters less than it seems, because the payment instrument is a SEPA mandate given at contract conclusion. The real problem is agent-collected mandate consent and its evidential quality, which no protocol addresses. Under PSD2, strong customer authentication still presumes a human; there is no separate regime for agents ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/agentic-payments-new-challenge-europes-payments-ecosystem)). PSD3/PSR is agreed but targeted to apply around 2028, and the extracts do not mention agent-initiated payments ([Worldline](https://worldline.com/en/home/main-navigation/resources/blogs/2026/the-scope-and-timeline-are-locked-in-for-psd3-and-psr-what-should-psps-know)). Stablecoin rails such as x402 serve machine-to-machine micropayments in USD and are marginal here ([Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-operational-launch-of-x402-foundation-to-standardize-internet-native-payments-for-ai-agents-and-applications)).

### Identity and sector plumbing: half built

Agent identity is splitting in two. Web Bot Auth, an IETF working-group draft, proves which operator's agent sent a request and explicitly says nothing about whether the request is authorised ([IETF](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol/)). The delegation layer (which human authorised what) is still drafts and pilots: Skyfire's KYAPay token, AP2 mandates, Verifiable Intent ([IETF](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-skyfire-oauth-kyapay-token/)). "KYA" means different things at Skyfire and Mastercard and is not one standard. Germany's state EUDI wallet is reported to launch with a basic offer at the start of January 2027, with mandatory acceptance by banks, energy and telecoms providers from 24 December 2027 ([Heuking](https://www.heuking.de/de/news-events/newsletter-fachbeitraege/artikel/digitale-identitaet-mit-annahmepflicht-eudi-wallet-und-european-business-wallet-im-ueberblick.html); [Börsen-Zeitung](https://www.boersen-zeitung.de/finanzen-technik/die-digitale-brieftasche-eudi-wallet-startet-halb-gefuellt)). It brings verified identity and free qualified signatures for natural persons ([Regulation (EU) 2024/1183](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1183/oj)), but an ITU workshop presentation lists a "digital power of attorney" as missing ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-T/Workshops-and-Seminars/2026/0330/Documents/Zoltan%20Pr%c3%a9csenyi.pdf)).

Sector standards favour energy. Since 6 June 2025 an electricity supplier switch must be technically possible within 24 hours on working days, triggered by the new supplier on the customer's authority; gas is not covered ([Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Vportal/Energie/KuendigungLieferantenwechsel/Lieferantenwechsel/start.html)). The 24 hours describe the back-end process, not the contract start ([Yello](https://www.yello.de/wissen/energiemarkt/24-h-lieferantenwechsel/)), and one supplier's CEO reports transmission errors and manual effort months after launch ([Tagesspiegel Background](https://background.tagesspiegel.de/energie-und-klima/briefing/warum-der-24-stunden-lieferantenwechsel-nicht-reicht)). Insurance has the B2B BiPRO standard and the FRIDA initiative, but the legal push is fading: the Commission's 2027 work programme, adopted 4 November 2026, lists the open-finance proposal FiDA for withdrawal within six months ([Commission Work Programme 2027](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/strategy-documents/commission-work-programme/commission-work-programme-2027_en); [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/financial-services/articles/2545816/eu-commission-to-withdraw-financial-data-access-proposal-draft-shows-update-)). It is not yet formally withdrawn. If it goes, no consumer or agent gets a legal right to pull insurance contract data by API; PSD2 account access stays the only statutory data pipe. Telco number-porting standards were not researched.

## What breaks and what survives when the buyer is an agent

### The funnel breaks first, and peers show how

Listed peers show a two-step squeeze. Organic clicks fall: a 2026 field experiment found AI Overviews cut organic clicks from 0.62 to 0.37 per search ([webcraftdev](https://webcraftdev.com/en/blog/google-traffic-drop-ai-overviews-diagnosis-2026)). Then paid acquisition gets dearer: MONY Group's CFO reported paid-search cost inflation above 20%, partly from AI Overviews, in the same half-year that it posted record revenue of GBP 227.1m ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/mony-group-h1-earnings-call-highlights-2026-07-21/?.tsrc=rss)). NerdWallet missed and cut guidance for a second straight quarter in November 2026 ([Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/417994/nerdwallet-misses-and-cuts-again-as-auto-insurance-weakness-overshadows-faster-revenue-growth)). MONY shares fell about 13% to a 13-year low on 10 February 2026 when Insurify launched a car-insurance app in ChatGPT ([City AM](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2398816)). Revenue has held up better than share prices. No public traffic, revenue or AI-share figures exist for Check24, Verivox or Idealo, so this pattern is imported from UK and US peers.

Four parts of the portal model erode. TV-built top-of-mind stops working on a buyer that does not watch TV. SEO and paid-search arbitrage shrinks as the answer moves into the assistant. Paid ranking positions lose value because lab studies find agents penalise sponsored labels ([arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630v3)). And UI steering disappears when there is no UI. These are the researchers' assessments, consistent with the evidence but not measured for Check24.

Agents also sharpen two old complaints. An agent that re-shops every renewal turns HUK's "switching machine" objection into a general provider problem, so providers will push for lower, retention-linked or trail commissions. That is speculation consistent with HUK's and Allianz's behaviour; Allianz also stopped paying Check24 commission ([Versicherungsbote](https://www.versicherungsbote.de/id/4879665/chapter/1/Check24-unterliegt-HUK-vor-dem-OLG-Koln/)). And with price parity gone in energy, an agent that can query suppliers directly will find prices Check24 does not have. Catalogue completeness becomes a credibility requirement.

### The execution layer survives, because agents still need it

The 2026 evidence favours the view that agents need intermediaries' supply, accounts and fulfilment. OpenAI's retreat leaves checkout and the customer relationship with the merchant or aggregator. In insurance, LLM platforms act as lead-generation surfaces for licensed comparers: Insurify serves real-time quotes in ChatGPT from 120+ insurance partners and finalises on its own site ([Insurify](https://insurify.com/press/news/insurify-expands-chatgpt-plugin/)); MoneySuperMarket's ChatGPT app lets linked account holders trigger a quote from stored data ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/technology/moneysupermarket-brings-aipowered-comparison-to-chatgpt-566181.aspx)). (Sources disagree on that app's launch date, 2025 or 20 February 2026.) General LLMs remain weak at live German rate comparison: a Biallo test found they often missed the best savings accounts ([Biallo](https://www.biallo.de/geldanlage/news/ki-zinsvergleich/)).

Five assets look durable; this ranking is the researchers' judgment. Licences plus liability-bearing advice come first, since insurance cannot yet be concluded inside ChatGPT in Germany ([Finanztip](https://www.finanztip.de/daily/versicherungen-per-chatgpt-veraendert-ki-jetzt-den-vertragsabschluss/)). Second, bind-and-execute rails into providers and the switch-outcome data they produce. Third, the logged-in contract inventory with renewal dates, which enables standing-mandate re-shopping that a stateless chat cannot do. Fourth, service-quality data (claims handling, rejection rates, bonus payout reliability). Fifth, negotiated tariffs.

Agent behaviour itself argues for a curated intermediary. In Microsoft's simulated marketplace, agents picked the first proposal 60-100% of the time, more options lowered welfare, and models were variably vulnerable to fake credentials and prompt injection ([arXiv 2510.25779](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25779v1)). Position effects stay "sticky" even when agents are told to ignore them, and model updates reshuffle market shares ([arXiv 2508.02630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630v3)). In negotiation simulations, buyers with weaker agents paid about 2% more ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/the-art-of-the-automated-negotiation)). All of this is lab evidence, not field data. It still implies that a consumer's general-purpose agent is better served by a small, verified, bindable shortlist from an accountable source than by an open bazaar, and that whoever supplies the shortlist holds the new ranking power. A platform can also sell that role as an ad slot, which is the central strategic threat.

## The design: a licensed agent that holds the household's contracts

Everything in this section is a design proposal. It is built on the sourced constraints above and on the researchers' design inferences; none of it is observed at Check24.

### Product concept: from "compare when you remember" to "kept optimal by mandate"

The product is a household contract account with an agent attached. The customer onboards once, the agent builds an inventory of their running contracts, and from then on it watches renewal dates, price changes and market offers, and either proposes or executes switches inside limits the customer set. The unit of value changes from a comparison session to a managed contract-year. The unit of trust changes from a ranking page to a signed mandate with a log.

Three choices define it. The agent is front-end-neutral: the same capabilities are reachable from Check24's app, from ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude, and from any third-party agent, because the 2026 evidence says the conversation will often belong to someone else. The agent is honest about its inventory: it shows non-commission tariffs and tells the customer when a provider such as HUK24 sits outside its catalogue, since a consumer agent that queries HUK directly will otherwise see a strictly larger market. And the agent optimises multi-year total cost and quality, not first-year bonus, which is both better advice and the only stance providers will tolerate (see risks).

Sequencing follows where delegation is most accepted and switching most mechanised: electricity and gas first, telco second, motor insurance third, with advice-heavy lines (disability, private health, mortgages) staying human-in-the-loop. Travel and shopping remain transaction verticals served through the standard retail protocols; they are not the heart of this design.

### Architecture: seven layers, two of which do not exist yet anywhere

| Layer | What it does (proposal) | Sourced constraint it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Agent-facing surface | One remote, stateless MCP server with OAuth account linking; an MCP App for rich comparison UI; an A2A agent card; UCP/ACP profiles only for retail verticals | Hosts expect one connector while Check24's verticals each own a server ([Check24 GitHub](https://github.com/check24-scholarships/check24-chatgpt-app-challenge)); stateless MCP core suits high-volume tariff queries ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/)) |
| Bindable quote service | Returns 3-5 normalised offers, each with a signed quote ID, validity window, multi-year total cost, eligibility pre-checked and machine-readable key terms | First-offer bias and "more options, lower welfare" in agent studies ([arXiv 2510.25779](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25779v1)) |
| Contract inventory | Per-household record of contracts, renewal and notice dates, prices and consumption, fed by PSD2 account data, document upload, broker mandates and GDPR access requests | No API right to insurance data if FiDA is withdrawn ([MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/financial-services/articles/2545816/eu-commission-to-withdraw-financial-data-access-proposal-draft-shows-update-)) |
| Mandate and identity | A text-form, scoped, revocable Vollmacht per vertical, stored as a verifiable credential; identity via eID today and EUDI wallet later | Cancellation authority must be in text form and presentable ([§312h BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312h.html); [§174 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__174.html)) |
| Advice and compliance engine | Generates the needs analysis, written reasons and documentation per insurance contract; archives and forwards all pre-contractual information to the customer | Broker advice and documentation duties ([§61 VVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vvg_2008/__61.html)) |
| Switch and cancellation orchestration | Long-running workflow: order new contract, capture SEPA mandate, terminate old contract, track the market process, handle rejections and overlaps | 24-hour electricity switch led by the new supplier ([Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Vportal/Energie/KuendigungLieferantenwechsel/Lieferantenwechsel/start.html)); telco switch and porting under [§59 TKG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tkg_2021/__59.html) |
| Policy and audit | Enforces the customer's limits, logs every action against the mandate, exposes a stop control and an action history | 93% of Germans want a stop function ([Lebensmittelpraxis](https://lebensmittelpraxis.de/handel-aktuell/47604-kuenstliche-intelligenz-deutsche-fordern-kontrolle-ueber-ki-einkaeufe.html)) |

**The agent-facing surface** has two tiers. An anonymous tier answers market questions (indicative prices, tariff terms, provider quality scores) for any agent and should be generous, because being the cited source is the new SEO. A linked tier, behind OAuth identity linking, releases personalised bindable quotes and accepts orders. Web Bot Auth identifies which platform's agent is calling; it does not establish authority, so the linked tier needs the mandate layer before anything binds. The surface must solve Check24's own stated problem: one endpoint, one schema family and one cross-vertical intent router in front of 90 P&Ls. That is an organisational decision more than a technical one.

**The bindable quote** is the product's core object and the answer to the protocol gap. A quote is a signed commitment by the provider, relayed by Check24, to conclude at stated terms for a stated person and validity period. It carries the multi-year cost with bonuses separated out, the term, notice period, price-guarantee scope, deductibles and exclusions in structured form, and a disclosure of Check24's remuneration. Check24 with broker status can already reach insurers through BiPRO-type B2B interfaces; wrapping these as tools is an inference with no documented production example.

**The mandate layer** is the piece nobody has standardised. The proposal is to build a proprietary text-form Vollmacht now, modelled on what German switching services already use, and express it as an SD-JWT credential shaped like AP2's open mandate: constraints signed once, executed later, with a non-repudiable trail. Its scope fields are the customer's rules: verticals covered, minimum saving, maximum term, provider white- and blacklist, minimum quality score, price ceiling, expiry. It migrates to EUDI wallet attestations and qualified signatures when those exist. An SD-JWT bridge between AP2 and the wallet is technically plausible; no source confirms one has been implemented. Check24 should contribute this mandate format and quote/bind/cancel/switch capabilities to an open standards body. A proprietary format would invite platforms to define their own; the French competition authority has already recommended that agentic commerce standards be developed through open processes ([Autorité de la concurrence](https://www.autoritedelaconcurrence.fr/en/opinion/competitive-functioning-artificial-intelligence-ai-agents-sector)).

**Orchestration** treats a switch as a multi-day task with failure states, mapped to MCP Tasks or A2A tasks, not as a checkout call. It owns the unglamorous cases: a rejected application, a failed market-communication message, a double contract, a lost bonus, an insolvent supplier. The outcome data from these cases is the proprietary dataset that makes the next recommendation better.

### Autonomy levels: approval by default, auto-execute earned

| Level | Agent behaviour | Where it applies at launch (proposal) |
|---|---|---|
| L0 Monitor | Inventory, deadline and price-change alerts | All verticals |
| L1 Recommend | Explained recommendation with remuneration disclosed | All verticals |
| L2 Prepare and approve | Everything prepared; customer confirms with one tap | Default for every customer |
| L3 Auto-execute in mandate | Acts inside signed limits after pre-notification, with a stop window | Opt-in; energy and telco first |
| L4 Full autonomy | Acts without pre-notification | Not offered for contracts |

The default is L2 because that is where German consumers are and where consumer protection sits. Stiftung Warentest advised against remind.me partly because it switches without the customer's active consent ([Stiftung Warentest](https://www.test.de/Strom-Wechselservice-Test-5447465-0/)). The Commission's Digital Fairness Act proposal of 11 November 2026 would, as reported, require agents that conclude contracts to let the consumer choose on first use whether to confirm every contract, only those above a price threshold, or none, and to set purchasing limits ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/european-commission-proposes-the-digital-fairness-act); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/ai-agents-dark-patterns-digital-fairness-act)). The proposal text was not retrieved and will not apply before roughly 2028, but building its consent model now costs nothing.

Trust UX has four fixed elements. Every action shows why, what it costs and what Check24 earns. Every auto-execution is preceded by notice and followed by a visible undo path, using the 14-day withdrawal right and the electronic withdrawal function mandatory since 19 June 2026 ([Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/vertraege-reklamation/kundenrechte/widerrufsbutton-seit-juni-2026-onlinevertraege-einfacher-widerrufen-118449)). Savings are reported after the fact against what the customer would have paid, not as a forecast. And Check24 guarantees its own errors: if the agent causes a coverage gap or double payment, Check24 pays. Insurance never reaches silent L3 at launch, because each contract needs an advice record or a separate waiver.

## Business model: keep the commission, change who it answers to

This section mixes sourced benchmarks with proposals and with the researchers' own arithmetic; estimates are marked.

### Each pure model has a documented failure mode

German energy switching already runs all three models. Wechselpilot charges the customer a success fee; its current official page states 25% of the expected saving on the first switch and 20% after, capped at EUR 200 per meter, where older pages said 20% of the actual saving after a year ([Wechselpilot](https://wechselpilot.com/ueber-uns/servicegebuehr); [Wechselpilot FAQ](https://www.wechselpilot.com/faq/wie-finanziert-sich-wechselpilot/)). SwitchUp and remind.me are free and commission-funded ([finanzherr.de](https://finanzherr.de/sparen/stromwechselservice/)). Stiftung Warentest's test of nine services does not rank cleanly by payment model, and it sets a benchmark: a commission received from the new supplier should be credited to the customer and shown in the offer ([Stiftung Warentest](https://www.test.de/Strom-Wechselservice-Test-5447465-0/)).

Per-switch commission rewards churn: a test report describes remind.me wanting to replace a contract after about five months ([test.de](https://www.test.de/gas-und-strom-wechselservice-test-5447465-0/)). Share-of-savings rewards inflated savings claims: Rocket Money charges 35-60% of first-year savings and reviews report fees on savings that never materialised ([Rocket Money](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/9744474-bill-negotiation-charge); [Motley Fool](https://preview.www.fool.com/money/personal-finance/rocket-money-review)). Share-of-savings also dies when price dispersion collapses, as UK auto-switchers found. Membership is stable but small: MONY's SuperSaveClub passed 2.1m members with ARPU of GBP 35 against GBP 20 for the wider base ([UK Investor Magazine](https://ukinvestormagazine.co.uk/?p=86534)).

### The recommended hybrid (proposal)

Commission stays as the base, with three changes. It is disclosed per offer. It is ranking-neutral: non-paying tariffs appear, and the ranking logic is published, which German unfair-competition law already requires of anyone offering a search across traders ([§5b UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__5b.html)). And providers are moved, by negotiation, from per-switch acquisition fees toward trail or retention-linked remuneration, which removes the churn incentive and answers the HUK objection.

On top sits a paid membership for standing-mandate management at L3, priced as a flat annual fee. In energy and telco, members get the commission credited against the fee or passed through, matching the Stiftung Warentest benchmark. In insurance that is not possible: passing commission to customers is prohibited with narrow exceptions ([§48b VAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vag_2016/__48b.html)), and a court has already ruled Check24's insurance "Jubiläumsdeals" inadmissible ([LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/lg-muenchen-check-24-jubilaeumsdeals-unzulaessig-rabatte-versicherungen-online-bkv)). Charging customers a fee for insurance work also raises the broker-versus-fee-adviser question under §34d GewO, which needs legal sign-off. Share-of-savings is used sparingly, only as a capped, after-the-fact success component, never as the main line.

A formal fiduciary duty is not law. Stanford's "Loyal Agents" work and Consumer Reports argue consumer agents in finance should owe a duty of loyalty, and Consumer Reports itself notes the term is not yet defined ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/designing-loyalty-ai-agents-and-conflicts-of-interest)). Check24 should adopt the duty contractually before a regulator or a competitor defines it. Given the 2019 cartel-office findings on commission-influenced rankings, a credible loyalty commitment is the brand asset it most lacks.

### Unit economics: what the sparse evidence supports

Savings headroom is large for inert households. About 22% of household electricity volume was still in basic supply in 2024 ([ZfK](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/vertrieb/mehr-wechsel-weniger-grundversorgung-das-wichtigste-aus-dem-monitoringbericht-im-ueberblick)). Verivox, a portal with an interest in large numbers, estimates a house leaving basic supply saves EUR 1,315 a year across electricity and gas, and that a cheap motor tariff saves about EUR 180 on average ([Verivox](https://www.verivox.de/strom-gas/nachrichten/54-milliarden-euro-verschenkt-deutsche-haushalte-zahlen-zu-viel-fuer-strom-und-gas-1121588/); [Verivox](https://www.verivox.de/sparpotenziale-familie/)). These are upper-bound marketing figures.

The researchers' illustrative arithmetic, to be read as estimates only: at 20% of savings, a basic-supply house yields about EUR 263 in year one, while an already-active household with EUR 150-300 of annual savings yields EUR 30-60, decaying after the first switch. If commissions are in the order of the old EUR 70-100 press figure, a household switching two or three contracts a year generates EUR 150-300 gross commission. Commission therefore matches share-of-savings for inert households and beats it for active ones, which is why abandoning it would be a mistake. The realistic baseline is far lower: MONY earns GBP 20 per active user per year because most users transact less than once a year. The economic prize of the mandate model is raising managed contracts per household per year, not raising price per switch. No verified figure exists for contracts per German household, Check24's acquisition cost, or LLM running cost per managed household; the researchers guess the last is single-digit euros a year, unsourced.

Gatekeeper cost is the decisive unknown. MONY's marketing margin of 57% implies roughly 43% of revenue goes to acquisition, on the researchers' reading of that term ([RNS via LSE](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/MONY/preliminary-results-for-year-ended-31-dec-2025-jsnb4yxgt860i2b.html)). The only disclosed agent-channel fee is OpenAI's 4% on Shopify sales through ChatGPT checkout from January 2026, whose current status is contested ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/ecommerce/2026/shopify-merchants-to-pay-4percent-fee-on-sales-made-through-chatgpt-checkout)). If platforms instead auction conversational entry points, costs converge on today's search economics. The mandate is the hedge: a household under standing mandate does not need to be re-acquired through anyone's ad slot at each renewal.

## Regulation: German law gates the binding step, and the gate favours licensees

Status claims below come mostly from law-firm and trade-press summaries seen as search extracts; paragraph-level wording should be verified before quoting.

| Regime | Status (Nov 2026) | Moat or obstacle for an agent-native Check24 |
|---|---|---|
| BGB agency law (§§164, 167, 174, 312h) | In force | Moat: the operator-as-representative model with a text-form Vollmacht is proven; obstacle: the customer must sign it and counterparties can demand it |
| §34d GewO, IDD, VVG | In force | Strong moat against unlicensed assistants; obstacle to silent auto-switching of insurance and to rebating |
| CCD2 | Applies from 20 Nov 2026 | Obstacle to full automation of credit: human-intervention right |
| PSD2 / PSD3-PSR | PSD2 in force; PSR around 2028 | Obstacle: SCA presumes a human; moat-neutral data pipe via account information |
| GDPR | In force | Obstacle (DPIA, purpose limits); lever (access and portability rights) |
| AI Act | Art. 50 since 2 Aug 2026; high-risk from 2 Dec 2027 | Light if the agent avoids credit scoring and life/health pricing |
| UWG §5b, P2B, DSA | In force | Obstacle to hidden commission bias; aligns with the proposed design |
| DMA, §19a GWB, Art. 102 TFEU | Evolving | Potential shield against platform self-preferencing |
| Digital Fairness Act | Proposal, 11 Nov 2026 | Would make approval thresholds law; no-regret to build now |
| EUDI wallet | Launch reported for Jan 2027 | Enabler for identity and signatures; no agent-delegation credential |
| RDG | In force | Boundary: cancelling is agency, disputing is legal service |

**Contract law.** German law has no AI-agent statute. An AI system cannot be a legal representative because it makes no declaration of its own; its acts are attributed to the user or operator ([§164 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__164.html); [Händlerbund](https://www.haendlerbund.de/de/ratgeber/recht/agentic-commerce)). Two constructions work. In the "tool" model the agent's clicks are the consumer's own declaration, which leaves the consumer with most of the error risk, since a poor choice by the agent resembles an uncontestable error of motive. In the "representative" model the operating company holds a Vollmacht and declares in the customer's name. The second is what switching services use and is the right basis here, because a cancellation by an authorised person fails if no proof of authority is presented and the recipient promptly objects ([§174 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__174.html)), and the authority to terminate a continuing contract on a switch must be in text form ([§312h BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312h.html)). It also places liability for bad switches on the licensed operator, which is where a trust product wants it. Open points: whether an agent-concluded contract via API satisfies the order-button rule, which assumes a human reading a button ([§312j BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312j.html)), and no German court decision on contract conclusion by an autonomous LLM agent was found. The EU AI Liability Directive proposal was withdrawn ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/proposed-eu-ai-liability-rules-withdrawn)).

**Insurance.** This is the strongest moat. EU law defines insurance distribution to include ranking and comparing products through a website where the customer can conclude directly or indirectly ([IDD](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/97/oj)). Intermediaries need a licence ([§34d GewO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gewo/__34d.html)), and brokers must ask about needs, advise, give reasons and document ([§61 VVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vvg_2008/__61.html)). Munich courts enforced exactly these duties against Check24 in 2016-17, holding it is a broker that must actively provide first information and meet advice standards online ([LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/lgmuencheni-37O1526815-check24-versicherungen-portal-online-makler-beratungspflichten); [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/recht/weitere-rechtsgebiete/allg-zivilrecht/check24-beratungs-und-dokumentationspflichten_208_417282.html)). The research notes give two different docket numbers for the appeal judgment. In 2026 the intermediaries' association Votum asked BaFin and the chambers to classify ChatGPT and Claude giving individual insurance recommendations without a licence, warning of a "liability vacuum"; no reply was found ([cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/unerlaubte-ki-empfehlungen-votum-nimmt-chatgpt-und-claude-ins-visier-718600/)). Until an authority rules, the licence is a distribution asset: the general assistant can talk, the licensed broker can bind. The documentation duty is also something an LLM does well, which turns a compliance cost into a feature. The same rules forbid a silent insurance switch with no advice record or waiver.

**Credit, payments, data.** The new consumer credit regime applies from 20 November 2026 and gives consumers a right to human intervention and explanation where creditworthiness assessment is automated ([Directive (EU) 2023/2225](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2225/oj)); loan brokering is licensed ([§34c GewO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gewo/__34c.html)). Credit placement therefore keeps a human escalation path. Payments stay on user-authenticated mandates; the agent should never hold banking credentials. Under GDPR, a provider-side refusal driven by a profile is an automated decision, per the SCHUFA ruling ([CJEU](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2023-12/cp230186pt.pdf)); a data-protection impact assessment is practically unavoidable; and onboarding should include authority to exercise the customer's access and portability rights against incumbents ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)).

**AI Act.** The agent is mainly a limited-risk system that must tell people they are dealing with an AI, a duty applying since 2 August 2026 ([IHK Hannover](https://www.ihk.de/hannover/hauptnavigation/digitalisierung/aktuell-digitalisierung/ai-act-neue-transparenzpflichten-gelten-ab-dem-2-august-7129608)). It becomes high-risk only if it scores creditworthiness or assesses and prices life and health insurance risk itself ([AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)); those obligations were postponed to 2 December 2027 ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/eu-ai-omnibus-enters-force-amending-ai-act)). The design rule: collect offers and pass data to providers who do their own scoring.

**Competition and platform law.** A single-answer agent makes hidden commission bias a higher legal risk, not a lower one, because ranking parameters and paid influence must be disclosed. On the defensive side, the DMA lists virtual assistants as a core platform service and bans self-preferencing ([DMA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/1925/oj)). ChatGPT reported user numbers above DMA thresholds in July 2026, but no designation decision was found ([eunews](https://www.eunews.it/en/2026/07/30/chatgpt-and-roblox-in-the-eus-sights-as-brussels-prepares-to-trigger-the-digital-markets-act/)). The Commission ordered Google in July 2026 to give third-party AI assistants access to Android features from 2027 ([European Commission](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/commission-provides-guidance-google-ai-interoperability-android-and-sharing-google-search-data-under-2026-07-16_en)). Idealo's first-instance award of about EUR 465m against Google, under appeal, is the precedent a specialist would invoke if an assistant steered users to its own commerce services ([LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/lg-berlin-zum-unfairen-wettbewerb-16o19519)). EU law gives sellers more room to block agents than the US Ninth Circuit's reasoning suggests, since terms of use can prohibit scraping where no database right applies ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/de/insights/besser-keinen-gesetzlichen-datenbankschutz-eugh-lasst-vertragliche-beschrankungen-von-datenbanknutzung-zu-wenn-die-datenbank-nicht-gesetzlich-geschutzt-ist)). Official APIs and partner agreements are the durable access path, which again favours the incumbent with provider contracts.

**Legal services.** Sending cancellations and switching on a Vollmacht is ordinary agency. Contesting a bill or a price increase needs individual legal assessment and is reserved ([§2 RDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/rdg/__2.html)). The Federal Court of Justice has read the collection licence broadly for legal-tech models ([LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/juristen/b/bgh-viii-zr-285-18-legal-tech-wenigermiete-de-inkassodienstleistung-weite-auslegung-abtretung-wirksam-rechtsdienstleistungsgesetz)), so a registered collection entity or partner lawyers are the route for dispute features. Whether an LLM reasoning about an individual case fits existing case law is untested.

The net assessment: German regulation is an obstacle to speed and to full autonomy, and a moat against the two most dangerous entrants, unlicensed LLM platforms and unlicensed startups. It is least protective in energy, where no sector licence was identified and anyone can run a mandate-based switcher.

## Competition: the contest is over who holds the mandate

**LLM platforms** are the gatekeeper threat, not the broker threat. They own the conversation, sell ads and sponsored agents, and have so far left the merchant of record with the seller. Their risk to Check24 is re-running the Google dynamic: charging for visibility. Their limits in Germany are licensing, liability and missing provider integrations; no evidence was found of LLM vendors building German provider integrations. Allianz is pursuing presence on platforms such as ChatGPT and a partnership with Anthropic while its CEO warns about dependencies ([Handelsblatt](https://www.handelsblatt.com/finanzen/banken-versicherungen/versicherer/versicherung-wie-die-allianz-vorreiter-bei-der-ki-transformation-werden-will/100236829.html)).

**Check24 itself** is the best-placed builder and its own main obstacle. It has the licences, the provider rails, the customer accounts and, in energy, the channel share. It also has 90 independent P&Ls, a model funded by per-switch commission, a history of commission-influenced ranking findings, and a bank under supervisory measures. The agent-native product cannibalises the repeat-acquisition revenue that the "switching machine" produces. An incumbent rarely makes that trade voluntarily, which is the opening for others.

**Verivox** was sold in March 2025 to Italy's Moltiply Group at an equity value of about EUR 232m plus up to EUR 60m ([ProSiebenSat.1](https://www.prosiebensat1.com/en/newsroom/prosiebensat1-verkauft-verivox-an-moltiply-514126)), against a reported purchase price of about EUR 170m in 2015 ([deutsche-startups.de](https://www.deutsche-startups.de/2015/06/29/das-bekommt-prosiebensat-1-fuer-170-millionen-euro/)). A decade produced little value for the number two; it describes its AI use as mainly internal ([cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/special-digitalisierung-kuenstliche-intelligenz-oder-doch-natuerliche-700325/3/)). As the challenger with less commission revenue to protect, Verivox has more reason than Check24 to adopt a customer-paid, loyalty-first model. Synatix, which took over Preisvergleich.de, names LLM-started comparisons as the emerging challenge ([ZfK](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/synatix-uebernimmt-preisvergleich-de)).

**Auto-switchers** (Wechselpilot, SwitchUp, remind.me) already hold mandates, the scarce asset, but only in energy; no evidence was found of comparable auto-switching at scale in insurance, telco or banking, and no customer numbers or revenue for any of them. They are acquisition targets or the seed of a rival. No funded European consumer household-contract agent was found, which is absence of search evidence only.

**Providers** will split. Direct writers with strong brands can refuse intermediation by agents as HUK refused portals. But visibility in assistants does not track market share: in one car-insurance test HUK-Coburg appeared in 3 of 18 searches against Allianz's 10 ([cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/ki-suche-veraendert-den-versicherungsvertrieb-vermittler-bleiben-gefragt-713143/)). A provider invisible to agents needs a feed somewhere. That gives an agent-native Check24 its one new argument to HUK: trail-based remuneration and multi-year optimisation instead of an annual acquisition toll. HUK's actual position on agent distribution was not found.

## Risks: the model fails when dispersion, trust or supply disappears

**Dispersion collapse.** The clearest precedent is UK energy auto-switching. Flipper announced its closure on 28 September 2021 because wholesale price rises removed the cheap tariffs it needed ([Money To The Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/quick-savings/utilities/flipper-review-is-it-the-best-energy-auto-switching-site)). Look After My Bills paused switching in 2022 and, per a secondary site without company confirmation, later stopped; savings were often under GBP 30 ([householdmoneysaving.com](https://www.householdmoneysaving.com/look-after-my-bills-review/)). The lessons are structural: spread across verticals so one market's crisis does not zero revenue, hold a revenue line not tied to switching, and screen supplier solvency. Regulation can compress dispersion too. The UK ban on insurance price walking almost halved the home new-versus-renewal price gap ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/corporate-documents/evaluation-paper-25-2-general-insurance-pricing-practices-remedies)). Germany appears to have no equivalent rule, an inference from absence in the sources.

**Provider retaliation and adverse selection.** Agent-managed customers are by construction the most price-elastic. Vattenfall stores customer data for five years to identify and reject bonus hunters ([test.de](https://www.test.de/Strom-und-Gastarife-Vattenfall-siebt-Kunden-aus-5859473-0/)). Expected responses are rejection, bonus exclusion, agent-channel tariffs and commission clawbacks. The design's answer is to prefer fair no-bonus tariffs, optimise over several years, and offer the incumbent a retention bid before switching away. No data exists on how many providers reject switching-service customers.

**Conduct and consent.** Over-switching (remind.me), alleged unauthorised switches (a Switchcraft user complaint on [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/switchcraft.co.uk)) and fees on phantom savings (Rocket Money) are the three documented ways these services lose trust. Mandate evidence, after-the-fact savings measurement and approval by default address each.

**Agent manipulation.** Shopping agents are "highly susceptible to indirect prompt injection" in constructed scenarios ([Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/retail-fraud-agentic-ai/)), and seller-side description tweaks shift share. No field data on agent error rates, hallucinated prices or agent-driven chargebacks was found, and no documented injection incident against a switching agent. Check24's own Sophie has already misbehaved in public, recommending bricks-and-mortar travel agencies and criticising online agencies ([fvw](https://www.fvw.de/touristik/vertrieb/ich-waere-vorsichtig-check24-chatbot-wirbt-fuer-konkurrenz--und-teilt-gegen-ota-aus-263932)). Binding actions must run on structured quotes and deterministic policy checks, with the language model kept away from the execute path.

**Quality hollowing.** If agents rank on price, providers cut cover; after the UK pricing remedy, motor compulsory excesses rose. The countermeasure is scoring on standardised quality data, which is why that dataset matters.

**Gatekeeper re-intermediation.** If assistants auction entry points, the paid-search dependency returns in a new form. Mitigations are the standing mandate, own-app usage, open standards and competition law. None is certain.

**Regulatory reversal.** A ruling that assistants may recommend and bind insurance without a licence, or platforms simply acquiring licensed brokers, would remove the main moat. Conversely the Digital Fairness Act could tighten consent beyond what L3 assumes. Both are open.

**Adoption.** The whole L3 business depends on behaviour only about 6% of Germans say they accept today. Revenue plans should rest on approve-mode conversion, with auto-execution as upside.

## A phased roadmap: earn the mandate before automating it

The roadmap is a proposal. Dates on external events are sourced above; everything Check24 would do is a recommendation.

| Phase | Timing | What ships | Gate to next phase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Be the source | Now to mid-2027 | One unified MCP endpoint and assistant apps across verticals; anonymous market data tier; published ranking logic and per-offer remuneration disclosure; contract inventory in the account | Agents cite and call the endpoint; inventory covers a meaningful share of active customers |
| 1 Bindable and approved | 2027 | Signed bindable quotes for electricity, gas and telco; text-form Vollmacht v1; L2 prepare-and-approve as default; automated advice records for motor; switch-outcome tracking and error guarantee | Switch success and complaint rates at or better than the manual flow |
| 2 Standing mandate | 2027-2028 | L3 opt-in for energy, then telco, with DFA-style thresholds; paid membership with commission credit; trail-based deals with first providers; EUDI wallet identity and signature once acceptance becomes mandatory (24 Dec 2027) | Paying members retained through a renewal cycle; providers accept trail terms |
| 3 Regulated lines | 2028-2029 | Motor on mandate with per-contract advice record and renewal-season automation; credit with human escalation; retention bids to incumbents; dispute handling through a registered entity or partner lawyers | Regulator comfort; PSR and DFA texts final |
| 4 Open network | 2029 onward | Mandate and quote/bind/cancel/switch capabilities contributed to an open standard; third-party agents execute through Check24 rails; wallet-held delegation credential if specified | External agent volume material |

Two moves should not wait for the sequence. The org decision to put one cross-vertical agent surface and one mandate above the vertical P&Ls is a precondition for phase 0, and Check24's own challenge document shows it is unresolved. And the commercial renegotiation with providers toward trail remuneration takes years; it should start while Check24 still has channel power to trade.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Will AI agents replace comparison portals?" is the wrong frame for Germany in late 2026: agents have taken over research and nothing else, the protocols they run on cannot express a contract switch, and the law reserves the binding step for licensed intermediaries. The live question is which party will hold the household's standing mandate when delegation does arrive. A platform holding it would turn Check24 into a supplier of quotes to someone else's customer. Check24 holding it would turn the platforms into one more front end. The mandate, the bindable quote and the contract inventory are worth more than the ranking page, and the first two do not yet exist in any standard, which gives a German incumbent a rare chance to define an agentic-commerce primitive instead of adopting one.

The uncomfortable implication for Check24 is that the agent-native version is a different company economically: fewer acquisition commissions per customer, more managed contracts per customer, revenue disclosed and partly paid by the customer. The evidence does not show whether German households will pay for that or opt into auto-execution; those are the two facts most worth buying with a pilot. Three things need checking before any of this guides a decision: Check24's real commission levels and vertical mix, which are undisclosed; the primary texts of the protocol specifications and the Digital Fairness Act, which were not read; and the regulators' answer on whether general assistants need an insurance licence, which decides how long the moat holds.
